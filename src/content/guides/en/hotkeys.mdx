## Direct answer: the fastest OpenFront controls

Use the [complete shortcuts reference](/shortcuts/) when you need to find a default key.

Use this guide to decide which controls matter first and rehearse them until camera recovery, building, fleet selection, and cancellation are automatic.

Press a number to arm a building or unit, then left-click its destination. The number row skips the radial menu, which is why experienced players can build or launch far faster than someone browsing icons.

For repeated nuclear launches, press `8` once for an Atom Bomb or `9` once for a Hydrogen Bomb. The nuke stays armed after a launch, so you can keep left-clicking targets until you cancel.

No autoclicker or external tool is required. Press `Esc` or right-click when you want to clear the armed nuke. Other buildings normally clear after one placement, so press their number again before the next build.

These are the default controls verified against the site's v33 reference. Since v24, keybindings can be remapped in **Settings → Keybindings**, and some actions can be left unbound.

On macOS, read `Ctrl` as `Cmd`. If a key on this page does nothing, check the in-game keybinding screen before assuming the action has been removed.

## Looking for Water Nukes instead?

Water Nukes is a lobby terrain rule, not a keyboard control. Use the [dedicated Water Nukes guide](/guides/water-nukes/) for conversion timing, attack choices, defense, and recovery. This page only explains the controls used to launch and cancel nuclear weapons.

## Concepts to understand before memorizing keys

OpenFront controls are context-sensitive. A left-click can attack a border, send a transport across water, or place an armed structure. The result depends on what is under the pointer and what you armed first.

An **armed build** is the preview attached to your cursor after choosing a structure or weapon. Number keys create that state immediately. `Esc` cancels it, while `Enter` confirms the armed item under the cursor.

The **radial menu** is the visual right-click build menu. It is useful while learning icons or checking upgrade options. Number keys are better once you already know which item you need.

The **attack ratio** is the share of troops committed to an attack. `T` lowers it and `Y` raises it. Those keys change the same decision represented by the on-screen slider.

Camera keys do not spend resources or issue attacks. Use them to keep your pointer near the active front while still checking your core, coast, and trade routes.

## Learn the number row as a workflow

Press the number, move the cursor to a valid location or target, then left-click. The number row is the highest-value group to learn because it removes repeated menu navigation.

Start with the actions you use under pressure: `1` for a City, `4` for a Defense Post, `6` for a SAM Launcher, and `7` for a Warship.

Add the other build numbers only after the sequence “number, valid location, click, confirm the preview cleared” is reliable. The [shortcuts reference](/shortcuts/#build) keeps the complete number mapping in one place.

`Enter` confirms the armed item under the cursor. `Esc` cancels the preview. When an Atom or Hydrogen Bomb is armed, `U` flips its launch arc between the two available directions.

The keys do not bypass game rules. You still need enough Gold, the required infrastructure, a valid placement or target, and any lobby option that enables the item.

## Make mouse behavior predictable

The default mouse scheme is contextual: left-click attacks, sends a transport, or places an armed build depending on the tile and current state.

Right-click cancels an active warship or boat selection in v33.1; outside that state it opens the radial or build menu.

You can reverse the menu behavior with **Settings → Left click opens menu**, so verify that option if your clicks feel opposite to this guide.

Do not confuse `Shift` + drag with repeat construction. The documented repeat behavior belongs to armed nukes. Cities, Ports, Factories, and defensive structures must be armed again after placement.

## Build a camera recovery habit

Use `C` as a recovery key. If a long pan or zoom leaves you unsure where your territory is, one press returns attention to your nation without hunting across the map.

`Space` is a reading tool rather than a cosmetic toggle. Alternate views can make either terrain or political ownership easier to inspect before you commit troops.

## Use combat keys only after checking context

Practice `T` and `Y` as a pair so you can change the attack ratio without moving the pointer off the front. Use `G` for a ground attack and `B` for a boat attack only after confirming the hovered target and route.

For fleets, `F` selects every Warship while `Shift` + drag preserves regional groups. The difference matters more than the input speed.

`K` and `L` were highlighted in the v31 release notes as faster alliance controls. They matter most when a diplomatic choice must happen without opening another menu over the battlefield.

Treat `Shift` + `R` with care. It targets the player who last attacked you, which is fast but less deliberate than choosing a border after checking the whole strategic picture.

## Decide between a hotkey and the radial menu

Use a number key when the decision is already made. If you know you need a Defense Post, pressing `4` is faster and leaves more attention for troop counts, borders, and incoming projectiles.

Use the radial menu when you are still deciding. It exposes the available build choices and secondary levels, which makes it safer for unfamiliar upgrades or a control layout you recently remapped.

Use the dedicated [shortcuts reference](/shortcuts/) when you need a compact lookup. Keep this guide for the reasoning, drills, and failure recovery behind those controls.

| Situation | Best control path | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One familiar structure | Number key, then click | Fewest actions |
| Several Atom or Hydrogen targets | `8` or `9`, then repeated clicks | Nuke remains armed |
| Unfamiliar upgrade | Radial menu | Visual confirmation |
| Lost camera | `C` | Returns to your nation |
| Fine attack-ratio change | Slider | More visual precision |
| Fast attack-ratio change | `T` or `Y` | Keeps pointer on the front |
| Several Warships | `F` or `Shift` + drag | Group control |

## A ten-minute practice routine

Practice in single-player so `P`, `,`, and `.` are available. The goal is not to win. The goal is to remove the pause between recognizing a need and issuing the matching command.

### Step 1: verify the default layout

Open **Settings → Keybindings** before the match. Compare the number row, camera keys, and attack-ratio keys with this page. Reset or note any custom binding that would make the drill misleading.

Also check the mouse setting. A reversed menu button changes the feel of every build exercise, even when the keyboard bindings are correct.

### Step 2: learn camera recovery

Pan away with `W`, `A`, `S`, and `D`, zoom with `Q` and `E`, then press `C`. Repeat until centering the nation becomes automatic.

Toggle `Space` while looking at a border. The exercise teaches you to change the information layer without moving the cursor away from the contested area.

### Step 3: build without the radial menu

Arm a City with `1`, place it, then press `Esc`. Repeat the sequence with `2` through `6` only when the item is available and affordable.

After each placement, notice whether a preview remains. Normal structures clear after one placement. That visual difference is what prevents an accidental second build.

### Step 4: rehearse border control

Place the pointer over a safe border. Tap `T` and `Y` while watching the attack-ratio control, then issue one deliberate ground attack with `G` or the normal border click.

Do not mash the ratio keys. The purpose is to pair each direction with its effect, then return to a known commitment level before the next attack.

### Step 5: rehearse naval selection

If Warships are available, use `F` to select all of them. Then clear the selection and use `Shift` + drag to select only a local group.

The two commands solve different problems. `F` is a global response; box selection preserves ships that should remain near another Port or coastline.

### Step 6: rehearse a nuclear launch safely

In a lobby where nukes are enabled, build the required infrastructure and save enough Gold. Press `8`, choose a valid target, click once, and verify that the Atom Bomb remains armed.

Move to a second target without pressing `8` again. Then press `Esc` and confirm that another click no longer launches the armed bomb.

Repeat with `U` before launch so you recognize the arc flip. Do this as a control drill, not as proof that one arc is always strategically superior.

## Practical action sequences

The sequences below are memory patterns, not universal build orders. Check your Gold, terrain, lobby modifiers, and threats before spending.

### Fast border reinforcement

1. Press `C` if the camera is displaced.
2. Move to the threatened border.
3. Press `4` to arm a Defense Post.
4. Place it where its 30-tile zone covers the relevant approach.
5. Press `Esc` if any preview remains.

A Defense Post starts at 50,000 Gold and gives `mag ×5` and `speed ×3` inside its documented range. The shortcut saves menu time, but placement still determines whether the bonus reaches the fight.

### Economy check without losing the front

1. Keep the pointer near the border.
2. Press `C` to inspect the core.
3. Use `1`, `2`, or `3` for the planned City, Factory, or Port.
4. Return to the front with camera keys.
5. Adjust the attack ratio with `T` or `Y` only if the situation changed.

This rhythm separates the economic decision from the mechanical execution. Decide the building first, then use the key to spend less attention placing it.

### Nuke salvo and clean cancellation

1. Confirm that the lobby allows nukes.
2. Confirm the weapon, Gold, and target.
3. Press `8` for Atom or `9` for Hydrogen.
4. Click each intended target once.
5. Press `Esc` immediately after the last launch.

The final cancellation matters. An armed weapon can turn a later click into an expensive mistake, especially while you are trying to pan, inspect, or switch back to ground control.

### Coastal response

1. Press `F` if every Warship must respond.
2. Use `Shift` + drag if only one fleet should move.
3. Pan with the middle mouse button or camera keys.
4. Keep the remaining fleet near the coast it still protects.

The v31 notes added multi-Warship selection and retreat behavior. Selection speed helps only when you avoid stripping every other lane of naval coverage.

## Numeric examples: speed is not the same as value

The shortcut is free, but the action is not. A City has a base cost of 125,000 Gold and adds 250,000 troop cap per level. Pressing `1` quickly does not make an unnecessary City a good purchase.

An Atom Bomb costs 750,000 Gold. A Hydrogen Bomb costs 5,000,000. A MIRV begins at 25,000,000 and adds 15,000,000 for each launch already fired.

That means one accidental `9` launch spends as much as six Atom Bombs plus 500,000 Gold. Always finish a repeat-fire sequence with `Esc`.

A Missile Silo costs 1,000,000 Gold, while the first SAM Launcher costs 1,500,000. The `5` and `6` keys accelerate placement; they do not remove the 2,500,000-Gold infrastructure decision.

The first Defense Post costs 50,000 Gold. Its cost scales with the number already built, up to 250,000. Fast access through `4` is most valuable when you have already identified the correct chokepoint.

## Common failure modes

### “I pressed the number and nothing happened”

First check **Settings → Keybindings**. The binding may have been changed or unbound. Then check Gold, required infrastructure, lobby modifiers, and whether the cursor is over a valid location.

Do not keep pressing the key without diagnosing the state. Repeated input can become a surprise purchase or launch as soon as the missing condition changes.

### “The next click launched another nuke”

Atom and Hydrogen Bombs remain armed after firing. This is intended repeat-fire behavior. Press `Esc` or right-click as soon as the planned salvo ends.

Build the cancellation into your muscle memory: weapon key, targets, `Esc`. Treat the last input as part of the launch sequence rather than optional cleanup.

### “Shift-click did not repeat my building”

The default reference does not document `Shift` as a general repeat-build modifier. `Shift` + drag selects Warships. Re-arm normal structures with their number key for each placement.

### “My click attacked instead of opening a menu”

Check the **Left click opens menu** setting and the tile under the pointer. The default action changes with tile ownership, water access, and whether a build is armed.

Press `Esc` before troubleshooting. Clearing the armed state removes one source of ambiguity from the next click.

### “Select all moved ships I wanted to keep home”

`F` selects the whole Warship force. Use `Shift` + drag when you need a regional fleet. Global selection is fast, but it can create an unguarded coast elsewhere.

### “Retaliate targeted the wrong strategic problem”

`Shift` + `R` answers the last attacker, not necessarily the most dangerous opponent. Use it only after confirming that immediate retaliation matches your larger plan.

### “I won the input race but lost the economy”

Hotkeys reduce execution time; they do not improve the decision itself. A fast low-value build still spends Gold and attention that could have gone to defense, trade, or troop capacity.

Use the [economy fundamentals guide](/strategies/economy-fundamentals/) to decide what to buy. Return here to make that decision faster.

## How to adjust against fast opponents

If a rival fires several nukes quickly, assume repeat-armed number keys before assuming automation.

Atom and Hydrogen Bombs can be intercepted by SAMs. The MIRV carrier is not targeted, but separated warheads use normal trajectory interception.

Use overlapping SAM coverage for valuable cores, but do not cluster every launcher in one blast area. The [nuclear deterrence guide](/strategies/nuclear-deterrence/) covers the defensive tradeoff.

If an opponent builds rapidly along one border, slow your own input down for one second. Check terrain, Defense Post coverage, and attack ratio before copying their tempo.

If your custom bindings cause conflicts, keep the most frequent actions close together. Preserve a clear cancel key, camera recovery key, attack-ratio pair, and the build numbers you actually use.

When a lobby disables nukes, the `8`, `9`, and `0` decisions disappear. Reallocate your attention toward camera control, Defense Posts, Cities, Ports, Factories, and Warships.

## FAQ

### How do players fire nukes so quickly?

They can press `8` or `9` once, then click several targets. Atom and Hydrogen Bombs remain armed after each launch. No external repeat tool is required.

### How do I stop repeat firing?

Press `Esc` or right-click after the last intended target. Verify that the armed preview is gone before returning to normal clicks.

### Does `Shift` repeat every building?

No. The documented `Shift` mouse action is box-selecting multiple Warships. Re-arm ordinary buildings with their number key after each placement.

### Can I remap all these keys?

The v33 reference confirms that every key can be remapped in **Settings → Keybindings**, and some actions can be unbound. Your local settings take precedence over this default list.

### What does `U` do?

It flips the launch arc direction of an armed Atom or Hydrogen Bomb. It does not change the documented blast radius or make the weapon immune to SAM interception.

### What is the quickest way to center the map?

Press `C` to center the camera on your nation. Use it after a long pan, a distant naval check, or a nuclear target scan.

### Should I use `F` or box selection for Warships?

Use `F` when the whole fleet should respond. Use `Shift` + drag when another coast, Port, or waterway still needs its local ships.

### Are pause and speed controls available in multiplayer?

The default reference lists `,` and `.` as single-player speed controls. Do not plan a multiplayer response around pausing or changing the match speed.

### Why does left-click behave differently on different tiles?

The input is contextual. It can attack, launch a transport, or place an armed build depending on the pointer location and current armed state.
