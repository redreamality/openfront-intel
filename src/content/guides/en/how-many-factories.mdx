## Direct answer: build two Factories, then stop unless the rail network is still short

Build your first Factory as soon as you have a rail line that can carry trains, and your second one when that rail line is actually crowded or the payout is proven. Then stop. A Factory is not a "more is better" building the way Cities are: it sits on the *same* cost ladder as your Ports, so every Factory you put down also makes your next Port cost 250,000 instead of 125,000, then 500,000, then a hard 1,000,000 cap. Your first two Factories pay for themselves if they feed a connected rail network and your unit count is still under the ~560 saturation midpoint; a third or fourth is a 500,000 or 1,000,000 bet on a game you can usually still win without it.

The number that should decide the question is your unit count, not the number of Factories on the map. The Factory's job is to raise the spawn rate of your train-produced units, and that rate is `max(1, floor((F + 10) × 15 / trainSaturation))`, where `F` is your total Factory levels. Push `F` up and the numerator grows, but the denominator (train saturation) is a curve centered on 560 units that climbs from about 0.25 toward a 0.75 plateau as your army gets big. So the same Factory level that buys you a lot of extra spawn rate at 300 units buys you almost none at 900. The decision is therefore: are you below the midpoint where extra Factory levels still translate into extra spawn, or above it where they mostly just burn gold? If you are below it and your rail network is the bottleneck, a second Factory is the right investment. If you are above it, a third one is spending 500,000 gold to buy a spawn-rate line you no longer need.

Treat the third Factory as a "win-condition" purchase, not an economic purchase. It is only right if the specific thing it produces — a flood of units from a saturated hub you already own — is what actually closes the game, and you can prove the rail network behind it can absorb the extra trains. Otherwise the gold is better spent on Cities (more income and troop capacity), a Defense Post, or a Warship. If your army is already large and your rail line is not the bottleneck, the second Factory is often the last one that pays, and the honest default is to stop there and redirect the remaining gold toward the thing the match is actually decided by. The rest of this guide is how to test that call with real numbers, where it breaks by mode and map, and what to do when the third Factory stops paying.

## Version boundary

Every number in this guide is pinned to the game state as of the **v0.34.24** release (published 2026-10-06), which was a troop-cap exploit fix and made no change to the Factory, Port, or train economy. The cost ladder, the 50-tick Factory build time, and the 560-unit saturation midpoint that this guide reasons about are all unchanged from the surrounding v0.34.x patches, so the advice holds for the version you are playing today.

The two version points that matter for the *story* of how many Factories to build are earlier. Trains and the gold-economy role of the Factory were introduced in **v0.33**; before that release the Factory did not have a rail-network payout to justify a second or third building, and "how many Factories" was a much smaller question. If you are playing a build from before v0.33, this guide's cost ladder still applies to the building price, but the train-network payoff that makes a second Factory worth it does not exist yet, so default to one Factory and re-read once you update.

The most important recent boundary is **v0.34.5**, which moved the train-spawn saturation midpoint from 400 units to 560 units. That single change matters directly to this guide: it means the "extra Factory levels still buy real spawn rate" zone now extends further into the mid-game than it used to, because the saturation curve stays low (and therefore the spawn-rate payoff high) up to about 560 units instead of 400. On a current build you can expect a second Factory to keep paying a bit longer into the match than the pre-v0.34.5 numbers suggest. If your match is on a build where the midpoint is still 400 (very old builds), a third Factory becomes a worse bet sooner, because you cross into the flat part of the saturation curve earlier.

Also note **v0.34.12**, which sped up mid-game spawns generally. Faster mid-game spawns raise the value of the Factory's train income during the window you are deciding between a second and a third Factory, which is one more reason the second Factory tends to pay off on current builds and the third one does not. If you ever replay an old save or a very old build, re-derive the ladder and the saturation midpoint from that build's config rather than trusting these numbers, because the cost ladder and the saturation midpoint have both moved across the v0.33-to-v0.34 line. Everything below assumes v0.33 or newer, and is exact for v0.34.x including v0.34.24.

## First, split the three different questions "build a Factory" usually means

When a player asks "how many Factories should I build?" they are usually mixing up three separate decisions, and each one has a different answer. Separating them is the fastest way to stop over- or under-investing in the Factory.

The first question is **commit**, which is whether you should invest in the Factory economy at all instead of staying a pure land-and-trades player. That is not a count question; it is a yes/no question about whether you will run trains at all, and it is answered by whether you can actually build a connected rail network, not by how many buildings you can afford. If you cannot commit to the rail network, the answer is zero Factories, full stop — and no amount of spare gold changes that. The commit question is covered in depth in the [Factory economy commit guide](/guides/factory-economy-commit/), which is where you should go before you spend the first 125,000.

The second question is **upgrade depth**, which is how many levels you should put into the Factory building(s) you already have — the x1/x5/x10 upgrade buttons. More levels on one Factory increase `F` the same way a second Factory does, and they move along the same shared cost ladder, so upgrade depth and building count are really two ways of spending on the same thing. The decision of *how to split* your Factory budget between adding levels and adding a second building is covered in the [Factory upgrades guide](/guides/factory-upgrades/), and the short version is: levels on a single Factory are usually the better buy while your rail network is short, because they are cheaper per `F` point than a second building.

The third question is **building count**, which is the one this guide actually answers: how many separate Factory buildings to put on the map. The reason the count question is distinct is that each new building is a fresh 125,000 → 250,000 → 500,000 → 1,000,000 step on the shared ladder *and* it also raises the price of your next Port. A second Factory is not just "one more Factory"; it is a 250,000 investment that simultaneously makes your next Port cost 250,000 more. So the count question is always really "how many total Port-plus-Factory buildings can my economy support right now," and that is the number the next section gives you. If you have already committed (first question) and are mostly deciding levels vs. buildings (second question), the count question is small and the answer is usually "one or two buildings, most levels on the first." If you are still deciding whether to commit, read the commit guide first and come back for the count.

## The shared Port/Factory cost ladder is the real budget constraint

The single most important fact for the count decision is that **Factories and Ports draw from one cost ladder, not two**. The price of the next buildable Port or Factory is `min(1,000,000, 2^n × 125,000)`, where `n` is your combined Port-plus-Factory level count (a Factory's *levels* each count, not just the building). That means the ladder is 125,000 for the first, 250,000 for the second, 500,000 for the third, and then capped at 1,000,000 from the fourth onward (because 2^4 × 125,000 = 2,000,000, which the cap clips to 1,000,000).

| Combined Port+Factory step | Price of that buildable | What you are buying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st (n = 0) | 125,000 | First Port **or** first Factory |
| 2nd (n = 1) | 250,000 | Second of either, whichever you choose |
| 3rd (n = 2) | 500,000 | Third of either |
| 4th (n = 3) | 1,000,000 (cap) | Fourth of either |
| 5th+ (n = 4+) | 1,000,000 (cap) | Further Port/Factory buys |

The consequence is that a second Factory and a second Port **cannot both be 250,000**: whichever you build first takes the 250,000 rung, and the one you build second takes the 500,000 rung. So "I want two Factories *and* two Ports" is really a 125,000 + 250,000 + 500,000 + 1,000,000 = 1,875,000 gold plan, not a "two of each at the base price" plan. That is why the count question is always a *trade-off between Port and Factory*, and why the [how many Ports to build guide](/guides/how-many-ports-to-build/) is the companion read: the same ladder governs both sides.

There is also a time cost the ladder does not show. A Factory takes **50 ticks** to build (a base of 5 build cost × 10 ticks), a Port takes **100 ticks** (10 × 10), and a City takes only **20 ticks** (2 × 10). So while you are building your second Factory you are out of action for half a hundred ticks, which in a contested match is often longer than the window the extra spawn rate is worth. This is a real reason the second Factory is a mid-game commitment and the third one is a late-game bet: by the time you have funded a 500,000 third Factory and waited through another 50-tick build, the match is usually in a state where the extra spawn rate no longer decides the game. If you are about to be attacked, or you are already behind on territory, the 50-tick build window of a second Factory is a vulnerability, and a City (20 ticks) or a Defense Post is the safer use of the same gold.

## Saturation: where extra Factory levels stop buying spawn rate

The Factory's value is its effect on the spawn rate of the units your trains produce, and that effect has a hard ceiling. The spawn rate is `max(1, floor((F + 10) × 15 / trainSaturation))`, where `F` is your total Factory levels and `trainSaturation` is a curve that depends on your current unit count. The saturation curve is a sigmoid centered on **560 units** (since v0.34.5) with a low floor of about 0.25 and a high plateau of about 0.75, and it is multiplied by an early-game boost of `min(1.5, 1 + (15 − units)/35)` that fades to 1.0 once you have 15 or more units.

What that means in plain terms: when your unit count is low, `trainSaturation` is near its floor, so the denominator is small and each extra Factory level buys a lot of spawn rate. As your unit count climbs toward 560, the denominator grows and the same level buys less. Past 560 and toward the plateau, the denominator is near its maximum and extra Factory levels buy almost nothing — the spawn rate is effectively capped, and spending another 500,000 on a third Factory buys you spawn rate you already have. This is the exact mechanism behind "build two, then stop": the second Factory is built while you are still on the low, productive side of the curve, and the third is usually built after you have crossed the 560 midpoint, where it is mostly a gold sink.

There is an early-game wrinkle worth knowing before you act on the saturation number. The `min(1.5, 1 + (15 − units)/35)` boost means that while you have very few units (under 15), your Factory's spawn output is boosted by up to 1.5×. That is why a Factory you build in the very early game feels disproportionately powerful — you are on the low side of saturation *and* getting the early boost. As your unit count passes 15 the boost fades to 1.0 and the only thing left deciding your spawn rate is the raw (F + 10) × 15 numerator against the saturation denominator. So do not extrapolate the early-game "my Factory is printing units" feeling into the mid-game: by the time you are deciding on a second Factory your army is large, the boost is gone, and the saturation denominator is doing the deciding. If you are checking your unit count in-match, the rule of thumb is: below ~560 units, a second Factory is still buying real spawn rate; above ~560, it is buying a line you mostly already have, and the third one is where the bet turns from "economic" to "win-condition."

## Numbered scenarios: when two Factories pay and when the third does not

The decision changes with your unit count, your rail network, and how much gold you have, so here are three concrete situations with the numbers worked out. Use them as the template for your own match rather than as a lookup table.

**Scenario A — mid-game, 480 units, one connected rail line, 300,000 gold in reserve.** You have one Factory at full useful levels and one Port, and your trains are running but the line is clearly the bottleneck. Your combined Port+Factory step is the third, so the next buildable (a second Factory *or* a second Port) costs 250,000. You are below the 560 saturation midpoint, so a second Factory is still buying real spawn rate. The call: build the second Factory now, because 250,000 against a still-productive saturation curve and a proven rail line is the best return available. Budget for it to also cost you 50 ticks, and hold a reserve of at least 50,000 for a response. This is the textbook "two Factories" case.

**Scenario B — mid-to-late, 700 units, rail network saturated, 550,000 gold in reserve.** Your army is past the 560 midpoint, so your spawn rate is near the plateau and an extra Factory level buys little. Your next buildable is a 500,000 step. The call: do **not** build a third Factory. You are above the saturation ceiling, so 500,000 buys a spawn-rate line you already have. Instead spend the 500,000 on two Cities (50,000 each plus the build) and a Defense Post, or on a Warship if the board is contested — the same gold buying territory income and safety, which is what a 700-unit mid-to-late position actually needs. This is the textbook "stop at two" case.

**Scenario C — endgame, 850 units, you own a dominant hub, the game is decided only by who floods units next.** Here the saturation argument says the third Factory is nearly worthless for spawn rate, but the *win-condition* argument can still win: if the only way to close the game is to produce a specific flood from a hub you control, and your rail network behind it can absorb the extra trains, then a 1,000,000 fourth-step Factory (or a very deep upgrade of the hub) is a legitimate endgame purchase. The call: build it only if you can name the exact objective it produces and you have verified the rail line and station range can carry it. If you cannot name the objective, the 1,000,000 is a losing bet and you should defend the hub and play out the lead instead. This is the only case where "more than two" is correct, and it is a win-condition purchase, never an economic one.

## Common failures and the counters

The count question has a small number of repeat failure modes, and each one has a direct counter. Recognizing which one you are doing is usually faster than re-deriving the whole decision.

The most common failure is **treating the third Factory as an upgrade of the first two.** Players who already have two Factories tend to read "one more Factory" as "a little more of the same," and spend 500,000 for a small spawn-rate gain they are too large to need. The counter is to check your unit count before you spend: if you are above ~560, the third Factory is a gold sink by the saturation rule, and the same 500,000 is better spent on Cities, a Defense Post, or a Warship. If you cannot justify the third Factory with a named win-condition objective, the default is to build a City instead.

The second failure is **forgetting that a second Factory also raises your next Port.** Players who build two Factories and then need a second Port discover the Port is now 500,000 instead of the 250,000 they expected, because the two Factories already took the 125,000 and 250,000 rungs. The counter is to plan the *combined* Port-plus-Factory ladder, not two separate ladders, and to sequence the buys: if you know you need a second Port, build it on the 250,000 rung and put the second Factory on the 500,000 rung, or vice versa, whichever your network needs more. The [how many Ports to build guide](/guides/how-many-ports-to-build/) walks through exactly this sequencing from the Port side.

The third failure is **building the second Factory during a 50-tick build window while you are about to be attacked.** The 50-tick build (Factory) is a real vulnerability, and building it into an incoming attack often costs more than the spawn rate it will produce. The counter is the same as the [building timing guide](/guides/building-timing/) teaches: hold the gold and the 50-tick build until the immediate threat is handled, and if you are mid-attack the safe use of the 250,000 is often a Defense Post or a retreat buffer instead. The fourth failure is **building a Factory to a rail line that does not actually carry trains** — a Factory that cannot feed a connected network produces almost nothing, so the count is effectively one wasted 125,000. The counter is to verify the rail segment limits and station range from the [train network guide](/guides/train-network/) before you build, so the Factory you put down is the one that can actually produce.

## Mode and map adjustments, then the checklist and conclusion

The two-Factories rule is a default, and a few modes and maps move it. In **team games** the math is per-player, so you can run a leaner Factory count and let a teammate carry the train economy; the shared-ladder and saturation math is identical, you just do not have to win the whole game with your own Factories. In **No Alliances** the 50-tick second-Factory build is riskier, because you have no ceasefire to build behind, so bias toward the first two and only take the third as a deliberate late-game win-condition purchase. On **small or Compact maps** the territory window is shorter, so the mid-game second-Factory case (Scenario A) comes earlier and the third-Factory case (Scenario C) is rarer, because the game ends before a 1,000,000 fourth-step bet pays. On **large maps with long rail lines** the opposite holds: the rail network takes longer to build, so the second Factory is worth holding a tick longer until the line is actually connected, and the third Factory becomes a more plausible mid-game buy.

Use this checklist in-match, in order: first, check your unit count and decide which side of the 560 saturation midpoint you are on — below it, a second Factory is still buying real spawn rate; above it, it is mostly a gold sink. Second, check whether your rail network is actually the bottleneck and can carry one more hub's trains, using the [train network guide](/guides/train-network/) for segment limits and station range. Third, check the combined Port-plus-Factory cost ladder and make sure the second (or third) Factory is the better use of that rung than a Port or a City, using the [how many Ports to build guide](/guides/how-many-ports-to-build/) and the [tall versus wide guide](/guides/tall-vs-wide/) for the broader land-versus-liquidity call. Fourth, check the 50-tick build window against the threat you face right now, using the [building timing guide](/guides/building-timing/) to decide whether the build is safe to start. Fifth, if you are even considering a third Factory, name the exact win-condition objective it produces and verify the hub and rail line can absorb it — if you cannot name the objective, build a City or a Defense Post instead and play out the lead.

The bottom line is that "how many Factories" is really "how many combined Port-plus-Factory buildings can my economy support right now, given where I am on the saturation curve." For almost every player, on a current v0.34.x build, the answer is two: the first to start the train economy, the second while you are still below the 560 midpoint where it buys real spawn rate. The third is a 500,000 or 1,000,000 win-condition purchase that is only right when you can name the exact flood of units it produces and prove the rail network behind it can carry it. Build two, stop, and spend the spare gold on the thing the match actually needs — territory income, safety, or a naval threat — instead of a spawn-rate line you already have.
