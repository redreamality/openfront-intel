OpenFront v0.35 renamed your most blunt economic weapon and quietly changed who it hits. The button used to be called **Stop Trading with All**, and it cut your trade with every other player on the board, allies included. In v0.35.0 it is now called **Stop Trading with Enemies**, and it spares the players on your team and the players you have allied with, while it still cuts trade with everyone else you are not allied with. That one word, *enemies*, is the entire feature, and it is the reason the global embargo stopped being a diplomatic nuke you could barely fire inside your own coalition and became a tool you can use while your allies keep feeding you gold. But the label is doing a lot of work for you, and one detail about it will cost you gold if you miss it: the button does not check your relation with each target. It stops trading with every player who is not you, not a Bot, not on your team, and not your ally, including the neutral trading partners you have not allied with. Read the version boundary, the exact numbers, and the decision table before you press it, and use the verification checklist at the end to confirm your own build. If you are on v0.34 or earlier the button still cuts your allies, and the pre-v35 [embargo timing and trade control](/guides/embargo-timing-and-trade-control/) guide remains the correct reference for that behavior.

## What changed in v0.35 and what stayed the same

The clean, verifiable fact is this: the only engine change between the v0.34.25 baseline and the v0.35.0 tag, in the global-embargo path, is one added line, and that line appears in two places. The function that decides whether the global embargo can fire at all, `canEmbargoAll`, gained an `isAlliedWith` skip, and the function that actually applies the embargo to each target, the init loop in `EmbargoAllExecution`, gained the same `isAlliedWith` skip. In v0.34.25 both functions skipped only three groups: your own player, Bots, and anyone on the same team as you. In v0.35.0 and v0.35.2 both functions skip a fourth group, anyone you are allied with. That fourth group is the whole change, and there is nothing else: the cooldown, the fact that it is a separate command from the single-player embargo, the fact that it is a permanent manual toggle rather than a timed window, and the fact that it has no influence, capital, or port requirement are all carried over unchanged. The v0.35.0 release notes describe it as a rename, Stop Trading with All becoming Stop Trading with Enemies that keeps trade with your allies, and the tag source confirms the rename is real but the change is narrower than the name suggests: the single-player embargo command is untouched by the release, and only the global command gained the ally-skip. That distinction is the entire feature, and it is why the version boundary matters so much for your decisions. Before v0.35.0, if you had allied with a rival and then pressed the global button, the button would have cut the trade you were running with that same rival, which made the global embargo a blunt instrument you could barely use inside your own coalition without severing your own supply line. After v0.35.0 the button preserves that line, so you can embargo the rest of the board without embargoing your own economy, and the global embargo stops being a tool you fear pressing and becomes a tool you press deliberately. There is no partial or intermediate version of the behavior to confuse you with: the v0.34.25 tag contains no `isAlliedWith` check in the global-embargo path at all, so a v0.34 game never preserves ally trade under a global embargo, and a v0.35.0 or later game always does. If you are testing on a build that is neither of those, confirm the behavior in your own lobby with the checklist at the end of this guide before you trust any number here.

## The two embargo tools and the ten-second cooldown

OpenFront gives you two distinct embargo commands, and the most common strategic error is treating them as the same tool with two sizes. The single-player embargo targets one specific player: it applies a permanent embargo on that one rival, and when you are done you target the same rival again to stop it. It has no cooldown and no requirement of any kind. You can embargo one rival at tick zero and a different rival at tick fifty, because the single-player command does not check a timer and does not check your economy. The global embargo, the button this guide is about, targets every eligible player at once. It has a cooldown of `embargoAllCooldown`, which is 100 ticks, and at 100 milliseconds per tick in v0.35.2 that is ten seconds. That ten-second cooldown is specific to the global embargo: the single-player embargo does not share it, and using a single-player embargo does not spend your global window or the other way around. This is the opposite of what the button's name suggests, and it is the detail that lets you combine the two tools. You can embargo your single worst rival with the single-player command and, ten seconds later, sweep the rest of the board with the global command, because they are independent. The second thing that separates the two tools is persistence. The global embargo is a permanent manual toggle: when you press the start side it applies a permanent embargo, an embargo with the temporary flag set to false, on every eligible player, and that embargo does not expire on its own. It stays until you press the stop side, which removes it from every player it had cut. There is no built-in re-open timer on the global embargo. The 3000-tick, five-minute `temporaryEmbargoDuration` you will see in the config belongs to a different mechanism entirely: it is the duration of the *temporary* embargo that the game creates when you attack an enemy, not the embargo this button creates. If you conflate the two you will mis-plan your denial window, because the global embargo lasts as long as you leave it, not for five minutes and then reopening on its own. The practical consequence is that the global embargo is a state you set and then live with for the rest of the game unless you actively reverse it, which changes how you think about the cost: the cost is not a time window you burn, it is a permanent cut to every non-ally trade line that you have to consciously undo if you want it back.

## What "Stop Trading with Enemies" actually stops

This is the detail that the label hides and that will change your decision if you internalize it. The button says *enemies*, but the engine does not read your relation with each target before it decides who to cut. The skip list in the v0.35.2 source is exactly four groups: your own player, Bots, anyone on the same team as you, and anyone you are allied with. There is no line that says *skip players I am not hostile toward*, and there is no line that says *only cut players at Hostile relation*. The engine embargoes every player who fails all four skips, and that includes a neutral trading partner you are simply not allied with. So if you are trading with a player you have never allied with and you are at Neutral relation with them, the global embargo will cut that trade the same way it cuts a trade with a player you are at full Hostile relation with. The word *enemies* in the button is describing the intent of the feature, which is to spare your coalition, not describing the filter the engine applies, which is *everyone who is not me, not a Bot, not my team, and not my ally*. This has a concrete consequence for your decision. The global embargo is only a clean tool when the set of players you are not allied with is exactly the set you want to cut. If you have a neutral partner you are still trading with and you do not want to sever them, the global embargo is the wrong tool, because it will sever them anyway, and the single-player embargo, which targets one chosen player, is the tool that lets you cut the hostile lines and leave the neutral line open. The same logic runs in the opposite direction when you want the blast: if you are in an FFA with no allies and no teammates, the set of non-ally players is everyone, so the v0.35 global button behaves exactly like the old Stop Trading with All, cutting every partner at once, and the ally-skip has nothing to skip because there are no allies to skip. The moment you ally with even one player, the tool changes character: that one player's trade line becomes protected, and every other non-ally line becomes a target, so the global embargo becomes a tool you can use inside a coalition without cutting your own line. The [embargo retaliation](/guides/embargo-retaliation/) guide covers what a rival does when you embargo them, and that is the diplomatic cost of the neutral-partner cut that the button's label does not warn you about.

## The exact numbers behind the global embargo

Here are the numbers the decision in the previous sections is built on, all read from the v0.35.2 tag rather than from memory. The global embargo needs exactly two things to fire, and nothing more: the ten-second `embargoAllCooldown` must be clear, and at least one eligible player must exist, meaning at least one player who is not you, not a Bot, not on your team, and not your ally. It does not need 100 influence, it does not need a live capital, and it does not need a trade port. That last point corrects a factual error that an older v0.34 guide made: the engine has no influence threshold, no capital requirement, and no trade-port requirement on the global embargo, so a player with one Port and a small economy can still fire it as long as the ten-second cooldown is clear and there is at least one non-ally partner. The trade economics that make the embargo worth firing are set by the trade-ship payout, and in v0.35.2 that payout is a sigmoid, specifically 75,000 divided by one plus the exponential of negative 0.03 times distance minus the short-range debuff, plus 50 times the distance, multiplied by your gold multiplier. The shape of that curve matters more than the raw number: it is concave at short range, makes a sharp S-curve in the middle, and goes linear at long range, which means a short trade lane earns a small fraction of what a long lane earns and a trade under the range debuff earns very little. The strategic reading is that your most valuable trade lines are your long ones, and the global embargo cuts the long lines as hard as the short ones, so when you embargo a rival who is running long lanes you are removing their best income, not their worst. The cost side is set by the Port, and in v0.35.2 the first Port costs 125,000 gold, the second 250,000, each one doubling, capped at one million, so a Port is a real commitment and the trade network behind it is a real asset. The single-player embargo, by contrast, has none of these numbers attached to it: no cooldown, no requirement, a permanent cut on one chosen player. So the cost of the global embargo is not a number you pay, it is a state you enter, a permanent cut to every non-ally line, and the only number that gates it is the ten seconds between sweeps. If you want to check your own build, the numbers to look for are the `embargoAllCooldown` of 100 ticks and the Port base of 125,000, both of which are in the engine config and both of which are unchanged from the v0.34.25 baseline, so if your build shows a different value you are not on the v0.35.2 numbers in this guide.

## Scenario: cutting a runaway FFA leader's trade network

Assume a Hard FFA game at minute 60 where you are the second-place economy and the leader has six trade-partner lines: three long lanes that carry most of their gold, and three short lanes that carry a small amount. You have one Port and the leader has six, so the leader's trade income is much larger than yours, and you are losing the gold race by a wide margin. You have no allies and no teammates, so in this FFA the v0.35 global button behaves exactly like the old Stop Trading with All, cutting every partner at once, and the ally-skip has nothing to skip. You fire the global embargo. Because the global embargo is a permanent cut and not a timed window, the leader's trade income does not drop for five minutes and then come back: it drops to nearly zero for as long as you leave the embargo on, and the leader's trade-ship payout, which was dominated by the long lanes, falls with the long lanes. Because you are the second-place economy, the hit to the leader is proportionally larger than the hit to you, since the leader was running more gold per second through those long lanes, and you buy yourself a permanent window in which the gold gap stops growing. The number that decides whether this is the right move is not a timer, it is the permanence: once you press the global button you have to be willing to leave the leader cut for the rest of the game, because undoing it is a deliberate action you have to take, not something that happens on its own. The relation risk is the separate cost: the global embargo itself does not change your relation with the leader, but if you follow the embargo by attacking the leader's Port, that attack drops your relation by 80 points on Hard, and a Port-attack at that relation level is a real war-declaration risk, so the global embargo is the cheap denial tool and the Port-attack is the expensive escalation, and the correct play is to fire the embargo alone and let the permanent trade blackout do the work rather than to chase the relation drop. The failure version of this scenario is the same game but where the leader's third place is also running a long trade lane with the leader that you are also trading with, so the global embargo cuts your line with the leader and with your other partner at the same time, and you have denied the leader while also cutting a line you wanted, which is the neutral-partner trap from the previous section showing up in an FFA. The counter is to check, before you press, exactly which lines the global button will cut and whether any of them are lines you want to keep, and if any of them are, use the single-player embargo on the leader and leave your other partner open.

## Scenario: embargoing a rival team while keeping your own team trading

Assume a team mode on Impossible where you and one teammate are allied against a rival team of two, and you have been running a trade line with your teammate that carries a large amount of gold while you run a separate line with one member of the rival team that you are trading with before the war. The rival team has decided to embargo you individually, cutting your line with them, and you want to deny the rival team their trade while keeping your own team line intact. This is exactly the case the v0.35 change was built for, and it is the case that was impossible before v0.35.0. You fire the global embargo. The button skips your teammate, because your teammate is on the same team as you, and it also skips anyone you are allied with, and it cuts the trade with the rival team members, which is the cut you want. Because the global embargo is permanent, the rival team's trade is cut for as long as you leave it on, and your team line stays open the whole time because the team-skip and the ally-skip both preserve it. The number that decides this is the asymmetry of the cut: you removed the rival's trade income while keeping your own team line open, so the net effect is a permanent denial against the rival at zero cost to your own team economy, which is the best case the feature can produce. The constraint is the ten-second cooldown, not a time window: after you fire the global embargo you cannot fire it again for ten seconds, so if the rival team has two members you are trading with the global embargo cuts both at once, which is exactly what you want, and if the rival team has only one member you are trading with the single-player embargo would have been the cheaper tool, but you have already swept the board. The failure version of this scenario is the same game but where the rival team member you are trading with is also allied with your teammate, so the rival is partially allied with your team, and the global button now skips that rival because they are allied, which means the button does not cut the line you wanted to cut and instead you have burned a sweep that spared the target. The counter is to check the ally-enumeration before you fire: if the target is allied with your team, the global embargo will spare them, and the single-player embargo on that specific rival is the tool that cuts them while leaving the rest of your coalition open. The [team trade coordination](/guides/team-trade-coordination/) guide covers how to build and protect the team line that the global embargo is now designed to preserve, and that guide is the companion to this section for the economy side of a team game.

## Failure modes and how to counter them

The global embargo fails in four distinct ways, and each one has a counter, so the failure list is the part of this guide you should memorize. The first failure is the neutral-partner cut: the button stops trading with every non-ally player, including the neutral partners you are not allied with and do not want to sever, so you cut a line you wanted to keep and the denial you intended is diluted by a cut you did not want. The counter is the pre-fire check from the previous sections: enumerate exactly which lines the global button will cut, and if any of them are lines you want to keep, switch to the single-player embargo, which targets one chosen player and leaves every other line, including the neutral line, open. The second failure is the ally-over-skip: the target you want to cut is allied with your team or with you, so the global button spares them because of the new `isAlliedWith` skip, and you have burned a sweep that spared the target while cutting the players you did not need to cut. The counter is the same pre-fire check, this time looking at the skip list instead of the cut list: if the target is allied, the global embargo will spare them, and the single-player embargo is the tool that cuts an allied target while leaving the rest of the board open, because the single-player command has no ally-skip and no relation filter and cuts exactly the one player you point it at. The third failure is the version error: you are on v0.34 or earlier and the button still cuts your allies, so the global embargo severs your own team line and the trade you were trying to protect, and the ally-skip does not exist in your build. The counter is the version check: confirm the button says *Stop Trading with Enemies* rather than *Stop Trading with All*, because the label is the cleanest single signal of which behavior you have, and if it says *All* you are on v0.34 and the entire math in this guide does not apply. The fourth failure is the permanence error: you fire the global embargo expecting it to last five minutes and then reopen on its own, but the global embargo is permanent and does not expire, so you are left with a permanent cut to every non-ally line and you forget that you have to press the stop side to get your lines back, and you spend the rest of the game with a trade network you do not want cut. The counter is to treat the global embargo as a state you set, not a timer you start, and to make a deliberate decision at the moment you fire it about whether you will keep the cut for the rest of the game, because the five-minute number you are thinking of belongs to the attack-created temporary embargo, not to this button. The [trade versus piracy](/guides/trade-vs-piracy/) guide covers the income side of the trade network you are either protecting or cutting, and it is the right companion for deciding whether a cut line is worth the denial it gives you.

## Mode and map adjustments

The global embargo behaves differently by mode and by map shape, and the mode change is the bigger one. In an FFA you have no allies and no teammates, so the v0.35 global button behaves exactly like the old Stop Trading with All: every partner is a non-ally, every trade line is a target, and the ally-skip has nothing to skip because there are no allies to skip, which means the global embargo in an FFA is the same blunt tool it always was and the v0.35 change does nothing for you there except rename the button. The moment you ally with even one player, the tool changes character: the button now preserves the trade you are running with that ally, and the global embargo becomes a tool you can use inside your coalition without severing your own supply lines, which is the entire point of the v0.35 change and the reason the change matters more in coalition play than in pure FFA. In team mode the button preserves the trade with your teammates and the trade with any player allied with you, and cuts the trade with everyone else, so the global embargo is the tool that cuts the enemy team's trade while your team's trade network stays intact, and the [team trade coordination](/guides/team-trade-coordination/) guide covers how to build and protect that network. On maps with many landmasses and short water lanes, the trade network is dense and the lanes are short, so the sigmoid payout means the total trade income is lower per lane and there are more partner lines to cut, which makes the global embargo a bigger relative hit on the board and more likely to trigger the neutral-partner trap, because there are more lines you might want to keep and the button will cut them all. On maps with few landmasses and long water lanes, the trade network is sparse and the lanes are long, so the sigmoid payout means the total trade income is higher per lane and there are fewer partner lines, which makes the global embargo a cleaner hit, because there are fewer lines to cut and fewer of them are lines you want to keep, and the ally-skip matters more because the few lines that are protected are the high-value long lanes. The one thing that does not change by mode or by map is the ten-second cooldown, which is the same in every mode and every map, so the cooldown math is the stable part of the decision and the mode and map shape change which tool, global or single-player, is the right call for a given board. The pre-v35 [embargo timing and trade control](/guides/embargo-timing-and-trade-control/) guide covers the lane-level decision in the old world, and this section is the v0.35 answer for how the mode and the map shape change which tool is the right call now.

## Decision table and how to verify in your own lobby

The table below is the decision rule for when to fire the global embargo, when to use the single-player embargo instead, and when to hold off. It is the pre-fire check turned into a lookup, and the row that matches your situation is the tool to use.

| Situation | Tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You are in an FFA with no allies or teammates and the target is the economy threat | Global embargo | Every line is a non-ally line, so the button cuts everything and the ally-skip has nothing to skip |
| You have a neutral partner you are not allied with and you do not want to cut them | Single-player embargo | The global button cuts the neutral line too, so the single-player cut is the targeted denial |
| The target you want to cut is allied with you or your team | Single-player embargo | The global button spares allied players, so the single-player cut is the only tool that reaches them |
| You are on v0.34 or earlier and the button says Stop Trading with All | Hold off | The ally-skip does not exist, so the button is the old tool and the v0.35 math does not apply |
| You want to embargo the whole board and leave it cut for the rest of the game | Global embargo | The global embargo is permanent, so it is the tool for a standing cut you will not reverse |

The table is the framework in one view, and the rows are the pre-fire checks from the previous sections turned into a decision. The first row is the ideal case for the global embargo: you are in an FFA so every line is a non-ally line, the ally-skip has nothing to do, and the button is the same blunt tool it always was, so if the target is the economy threat the global cut is the right call. The second row is the case the label hides: you have a neutral partner you are not allied with, and the global button will cut that partner the same as a hostile one, so if you do not want to sever them the single-player embargo is the tool that cuts the hostile lines and leaves the neutral line open. The third row is the case the new skip creates: the target you want to cut is allied with you or your team, so the global button spares them and the single-player embargo is the only tool that reaches an allied target, because the single-player command has no ally-skip and no relation filter. The fourth row is the version error: you are on v0.34 and the button still says *All*, so the ally-skip does not exist and the entire v0.35 math does not apply to your build. The fifth row is the permanence question: if you want to embargo the whole board and leave it cut for the rest of the game, the global embargo is the tool, because it is permanent and does not expire, so it is the tool for a standing cut you do not intend to reverse. None of this should be taken on faith, so here is a way to confirm the behavior in your own lobby before you commit. First, confirm the version with the button label: v0.34 says *Stop Trading with All* and v0.35.0 and later say *Stop Trading with Enemies*, so the label is the cleanest single signal of which behavior you have. Second, confirm the ally-skip: ally with a test rival who has a trade line with you, fire the global embargo, and watch whether the trade line with that ally stays open, because on v0.35.0 and later the line stays open and on v0.34 the line is cut, which is the cleanest single behavior to confirm the version. Third, confirm the neutral cut: with a test rival you are not allied with and at Neutral relation, fire the global embargo and watch whether the neutral line is cut, because the button cuts it even though the label says *enemies*. Fourth, confirm the cooldown: fire the global embargo and immediately try it again, and the button should be unavailable for ten seconds, which is the behavior that proves the ten-second cooldown is real. Fifth, confirm the permanence: fire the global embargo on a hostile partner with a trade line, and watch the line stay cut after five minutes, because the global embargo does not expire and the line reopens only when you press the stop side. If any of those checks fails, the game is not behaving like v0.35.2 and the numbers in this guide do not apply to that build, which is exactly why the verification step is part of the guide and not an afterthought. Keep the [first match](/guides/first-match/) guide handy for the early-game economic setup that has to exist before a Port is even affordable, and the [trade versus piracy](/guides/trade-vs-piracy/) guide for the income-side math that tells you why a trade line is worth protecting in the first place.
