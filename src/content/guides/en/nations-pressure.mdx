## Direct answer: stop becoming the best safe prize

Against a Hard or Impossible Nation, expand only while the next capture improves your position without turning you into its best safe prize. Keep one defensible contact, let the Nation spend elsewhere, then use a bounded attack that leaves a declared reserve. Stop when your troop count drops, valuable structures become exposed, or a second hostile border opens; refill, fortify, or change routes before attacking again.

That answer matters more in OpenFront `v0.34.0-beta1`. The official Release added the `juicy()` attack strategy: Hard and Impossible Nations can choose a valuable bordering rival they can safely beat instead of mechanically selecting the lowest current troop count. The eligible pool is narrower than the name suggests. A candidate must have current Troops no greater than 75% of the Nation's current Troops. Eligible candidates are then compared on three equally added, min-max-normalized signals: levels of capturable structures, unused troop-cap headroom, and owned tiles. Defense Posts and Missile Silos are excluded from the structure prize score. The code does not publish a visible "juiciness" number, so use those inputs as a risk board, not as a calculator that predicts the next click.

The new strategy sits inside a larger priority list. Hard checks Bots, retaliation, ally assistance, betrayal, fallout, traitors, disconnected players, hostility, and very weak targets before `juicy`; Impossible puts retaliation, Bots, very weak targets, betrayal, assistance, and an already pressured victim before it. Unowned land is handled even earlier. A Nation can therefore ignore the apparent best prize because another branch succeeds first. It can also wait: each Nation has a generated reserve ratio between 30% and 39% of maximum Troops and a generated normal trigger between 50% and 59%. Below the reserve it does not enter ordinary target selection, while below the trigger it usually waits but retains a one-in-ten continuation chance.

Use **P-R-E-S-S: Pressure, Reward, Exposure, Stop line, Switch**. Pressure asks whether the Nation can legally and safely send a meaningful attack. Reward asks whether your structures, cap headroom, and land make you attractive. Exposure counts the borders and routes that can punish the move. Stop line states the visible event that ends expansion. Switch chooses refill, economy, Defense Post coverage, diplomacy, another route, or a limited counterattack. Run the five checks before a capture and again after it creates a new border.

This guide owns the player decision around Nation pressure. [Nation AI mechanics](/mechanics/nations/) remains the reference for the broader behavior tree, structures, alliances, ships, and nuclear branches. [Threat Assessment](/guides/threat-assessment/) scans every rival, while this page narrows that scan to a Hard or Impossible Nation border. [Land Combat](/guides/land-combat/) explains how an attack resolves after you choose it. Here the completion test is simpler: you can name why the next move does not make you the most valuable safe adjacent target, and you already know what signal will make you stop.

## Read v34 pressure without pretending it is deterministic

Start with what the Nation can actually reach. The `juicy()` candidates are bordering enemies, built from direct land contacts and the engine's nearby-player relationship. A rich island across open water is not automatically in that candidate set, though separate random-boat and island strategies can still create naval pressure. A player behind an active friendly relation is filtered out of the enemy list, but alliance expiry, hostility, betrayal rules, and Team relationships can change that classification. The map is not a static heat map: every capture can add a candidate, remove a buffer, or turn a compact edge into two active contacts.

Next separate **eligibility** from **preference**. If a Nation has 1,000,000 current Troops, `juicy()` accepts only bordering enemies at 750,000 or below. A player at 760,000 does not qualify for that branch, even with many Cities. A player at 700,000 may qualify, but is not guaranteed to win the score. Structure levels count, so three level-3 Cities contribute more prize value than three level-1 Cities. The unused-cap signal is `1 - current Troops / maximum Troops`, and land is the raw number of owned tiles. Each signal is normalized only among eligible candidates before the three values are added. No published weight makes one City worth a fixed tile count across every border.

The other branches prevent a single-rule exploit. A rival below 15% of its own maximum can satisfy the very-weak search, provided it is not too strong relative to the Nation. A rival receiving incoming attacks totaling more than half its current Troops can satisfy the victim search, again subject to the FFA strength boundary. A hostile relation, traitor status, a disconnected player, ally assistance, or retaliation can arrive before or after `juicy` depending on difficulty. Do not try to become "unjuicy" by sitting at 76% of the Nation if you are simultaneously very weak relative to your own cap or actively attacking it.

Finally read the send cap. In ordinary FFA, Hard generally preserves Troops equal to 75% of the strongest non-friendly neighboring human force; Impossible preserves 90%. Hard and Impossible also reject a normal attack when the capped send would be below 20% of the target's current Troops. These are Nation restraints, not player reserve recommendations. They are bypassed in Team mode, and an incoming attack can make the Nation raise its usable response to at least the total incoming force. The v34 fix also keeps the cap relevant when attacking Terra Nullius or fallout instead of letting expansion ignore it.

The practical board read is probabilistic. Mark a Nation as **cold** when it lacks reserve, has attractive neutral land, or is committed elsewhere; **warm** when you are eligible and your border value is rising; **hot** when you are eligible, underfilled, structure-rich, and the Nation has both Troops and a clean contact. Those labels do not expose internal random state. They tell you how much reversibility the next move needs. A cold border permits a probe. A warm border requires an explicit stop line. A hot border should be shortened or allowed to cool before another permanent investment is placed there.

## Run P-R-E-S-S before and after every border change

**Pressure** is the Nation's ability to act now, not its difficulty label alone. Record its visible current Troops, whether it is refilling or already sending attacks, whether free land touches it, and whether another player is hitting it. Hard evaluates attack behavior every generated 45 to 59 ticks; Impossible every 30 to 49 ticks. Those intervals explain faster reactions but are not countdowns you can safely race, because the starting offset is generated and other behaviors share the cycle. Treat a visible outgoing attack as a window whose size depends on the force actually committed, not as a fixed number of seconds.

**Reward** is the part v34 makes newly important. Count your capturable structure levels other than Defense Posts and Silos, estimate how underfilled you are compared with your own maximum, and compare your land with other eligible neighbors. The correct action is not to demolish useful infrastructure or fill Troops merely to manipulate the AI. Instead, avoid placing the next City, Factory, or Port on an exposed Nation border when the same investment can sit behind depth. If a valuable structure is already exposed, make the access expensive through shape, reserve, and coverage rather than assuming the structure itself deters the Nation.

**Exposure** counts responses. List direct hostile contacts after the proposed capture, a water route that can bypass the line, and any ally whose expiry would expose a side. One new strip of neutral land can be strategically negative if it removes a buffer and makes a high-value core adjacent. Prefer expansion that ends at a narrow, coverable boundary. When choosing between equal neutral areas, take the one that leaves the Nation touching a low-value outer layer rather than the one that opens a straight path to upgraded Cities.

**Stop line** must be a map event or a number visible during play. Useful examples are "stop when 650,000 Troops remain," "stop after the western neutral pocket closes," "stop if the Nation ends its other attack," or "stop when a second enemy shares my border." A universal percentage is weaker because maps, cap, structures, and opponent pressure differ. Write the stop before the first click. If the move crosses it, cancel reinforcements even when the first tiles look cheap.

**Switch** selects the next posture. Refill when low current Troops are the main risk. Build economy only when the construction remains behind a defendable line and will finish before the next needed move. Add a Defense Post when its 30-tile zone covers the real contact rather than decorating the core. Change route when another target creates less exposure. Use diplomacy when it removes a border long enough to complete a named action, but do not treat a friendly icon as permanent immunity. Counterattack only when the Nation's commitment or shape gives a bounded objective.

Rerun all five checks after the first contact. Reward and Exposure can change in one captured tile; Pressure changes when an outgoing attack starts or ends. A good plan can stop early without being a failed plan. Its purpose is to buy information while preserving the option to choose again.

## Scenario one: the richer target is attacked before the weakest

Assume a v0.34.0-beta1 FFA with one Hard Nation at 1,200,000 current Troops. It borders you at 780,000, a smaller rival at 420,000, and a third non-friendly human at 1,000,000. You own 18,000 tiles and three level-3 Cities near the contact. Your maximum Troops are 1,800,000, so you are at about 43% of cap. The 420,000 rival owns 6,000 tiles, no completed structure, and is at 70% of cap. Assume no neutral land touches the Nation, nobody is attacking it, no higher-priority branch succeeds, and all stated players are reachable enemies. These values are a teaching model, not a prediction for a named map.

Both you and the smaller rival pass the `juicy()` safety filter because each has no more than 900,000 Troops, which is 75% of the Nation's 1,200,000. The smaller rival has fewer current Troops, but you dominate the other three prize signals: nine City levels versus zero, much more unused troop-cap headroom, and more land. The official end-to-end test pins exactly this qualitative result: under Hard and Impossible, the structure-rich, underfilled, larger target can be chosen over the numerically weakest neighbor when `juicy` reaches its priority slot.

Now calculate the Hard FFA send restraint. The strongest non-friendly neighboring human in the assumptions has 1,000,000 Troops, so Hard normally keeps at least 750,000. From 1,200,000, that leaves a maximum normal send of 450,000. The 20% minimum against your 780,000 is 156,000, so the cap does not block an attack. This does not prove that exactly 450,000 will be sent; reserve logic, the selected attack ratio, other active attacks, and execution timing still apply. It proves only that "the weaker rival will be attacked first" is not a safe plan.

Run P-R-E-S-S. Pressure is hot: the Nation can allocate a meaningful attack. Reward is high because the exposed core has levels, land, and unused cap. Exposure is poor because your Cities sit near the only line. Set the stop line before taking more neutral land: do not cross the tile that exposes the City cluster, and stop any current expansion when 900,000 Troops remain. Switch to refill and shorten the contact. If a Defense Post can cover the actual route, place it behind enough depth to finish; if not, preserve Gold and make the outer strip the only first prize.

The opponent counterplay is to attack you at the same moment, keeping your cap underfilled and turning you into both a `juicy` candidate and a victim with heavy incoming pressure. Your defense is not to full-send at the Nation to escape the score. That creates retaliation, may bypass its normal restraint, and drains the reserve. Instead, detach from the third player's fight where possible, let the Nation choose or spend, and counter only after its outgoing force is visible. Success means the Cities remain supplied and you retain another decision; it does not require conquering the Nation immediately.

## Scenario two: wait for commitment, then take one bounded objective

Assume an Impossible Nation has 1,500,000 current Troops and borders two humans. You have 1,050,000 current Troops, exactly 70% of the Nation, a compact 12,000-tile position, one level-2 City behind the border, and 600,000 Gold. The other human has 1,300,000. A neutral corridor is already exhausted. The Nation begins an 500,000-Troop attack against the other human, leaving about 1,000,000 visible before combat changes it. Your objective is not elimination; it is a 2,500-tile neck that shortens your border and places the City two layers behind the front. Assume ordinary FFA, no alliance changes, no other incoming attack, and a land route without impassable terrain.

Before the Nation commits, you pass the 75% `juicy` candidate boundary because 1,050,000 is below 1,125,000. You are not automatically selected, but the structure, land, and headroom make passivity risky. Impossible's normal FFA restraint looks at its strongest relevant neighboring human and tries to preserve 90% of that force. With the other player at 1,300,000, the floor is 1,170,000; at 1,500,000 current Troops, only about 330,000 is normally available. An attack against your 1,050,000 needs at least 210,000 to pass the 20% weak-attack check, so pressure is possible but bounded under the assumptions.

After the visible 500,000 commitment, do not simply subtract and full-send. The outgoing attack may resolve, the other human's count may fall, and the Nation's cap is recomputed from live state. Instead, use the commitment as the trigger for a fixed objective. Allocate a first push small enough that at least 800,000 remains at home; stop at the neck even if adjacent tiles look open. The 800,000 figure is an explicit scenario assumption chosen to answer one counterattack, not a universal recommendation. Do not start the City or another exposed building during the probe. Reward should decline as the line moves away from the existing City, not increase through fresh construction.

The stop line has three parts: the Nation cancels or finishes its other attack, your reserve reaches 800,000, or the neck opens a second hostile contact. At the first signal, Switch. If the neck is already secure, hold and refill. If it is only a salient, abandon further capture rather than feeding it. If the other human counterattacks the Nation and keeps its force occupied, reinforce only enough to complete the shorter line. Recheck the Nation's current Troops after every material change because the 75% eligibility boundary moves with it.

In Team mode, this arithmetic cannot be carried over. The strongest-neighbor reserve floor and the below-20% rejection are FFA-only in the official tests. A Nation under attack can also raise its response to at least total incoming Troops. That means a coordinated team full-send may provoke a much larger answer than the solo FFA window suggests. The scenario is complete when the neck is taken or intentionally abandoned while the core remains able to answer pressure. Eliminating the Nation is a later decision requiring another P-R-E-S-S pass.

## Failure modes and how a Nation or human rival punishes them

The first failure is treating 75% as a personal safety threshold. It is only the `juicy()` candidate ceiling relative to the Nation's current Troops. Sitting at 76% may avoid that branch for one evaluation, but retaliation, hostility, victim, very-weak, betrayal, or weakest-target logic can still select you. A Nation's current count also changes, moving the line without your action. The recovery is to monitor visible commitment and border shape, not camp on one calculated ratio.

The second failure is lowering visible value by spending recklessly. Filling troop capacity, building an exposed Defense Post, or attacking a random neighbor to change a ratio can make the actual position worse. Defense Posts and Missile Silos do not add to the `juicy` structure score, but they still cost Gold, need construction time, and can be captured or bypassed. A Defense Post is justified when its 30-tile effect covers the likely approach and the core can protect construction. A Silo is a strategic system, not an AI-decoy button. Reduce exposure before manipulating a score you cannot see.

The third failure is attacking to "keep the Nation busy." Incoming pressure activates retaliation and can relax normal limits. Official v34 tests show that a Hard Nation whose ordinary cap would be zero can respond with at least the total incoming force. In Team, the FFA reserve floor and weak-attack rejection do not protect the attacker. Human opponents can exploit this by waiting for you to provoke the Nation, then entering your drained flank. The safer intervention is a bounded objective after a visible commitment, with no reinforcement past the declared stop.

The fourth failure is reading every pause as friendship. A Nation below its generated 30%-39% reserve waits; one below its 50%-59% trigger usually waits; one with neutral land may expand first; one with a higher-priority target may pass you temporarily. None of those states promises a lasting truce. An alliance can remove you from the current enemy list, but expiry and betrayal have their own checks. Use the quiet period to finish a named improvement, then reassess before the relation or board changes.

The fifth failure is overbuilding the prize corridor. Cities, Factories, and Ports should usually sit behind the line they support. A Nation's score counts capturable structure levels but excludes Defense Posts and Silos. A human rival does not share that exact scoring rule and may value any captured infrastructure. Keep the interpretation player-centered: the model tells you why exposed growth can attract Hard or Impossible; it does not say that hiding all structures wins. Build where the return survives a border loss.

| Bad shortcut | Why it fails in v34 | Recoverable replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "Stay above 75% and I am safe" | The line belongs only to `juicy`, moves with Nation Troops, and other branches remain | Track commitment, relation, cap fill, and border geometry |
| "The weakest neighbor goes first" | Valuable land, structure levels, and headroom can outrank raw current Troops | Compare all eligible neighbors and reduce exposed reward |
| "Provoke a small counterattack" | Retaliation and Team exceptions can bypass ordinary restraints | Wait for an existing commitment, then take one bounded objective |
| "More border means more options" | New contact may expose the economic core and add a second attacker | End expansion at a narrow line with a prewritten stop |

## Adjust the loop for maps, FFA, Team, and Humans versus Nations

On a crowded continental map, Exposure dominates. Italy, central Europe, India, and similar junctions can turn a small neutral capture into several Nation contacts. Do not convert community map anecdotes into guaranteed spawn advice; map version, player count, Nation placement, and settings change the board. Use the topological lesson instead: prefer an edge or choke point that limits reachable enemies, and stop neutral expansion before it erases the last buffer around valuable Cities. If two captures are equal in area, choose the one that leaves fewer direct hostile neighbors.

On islands and peninsulas, Pressure arrives differently. A lack of land contact removes the immediate bordering pool but does not disable random boats or the separate island strategy. A safe island buys time only if its economy creates a route and its coast can survive a Transport Ship. Do not overinvest in a Port simply because community videos won from Australia, New Zealand, Greenland, or Antarctica. Validate the actual water component, landing coast, Warship screen, and follow-up path. [Island Defense](/guides/island-defense/) handles the defender's coastal layer; P-R-E-S-S resumes when the first land contact exists.

In ordinary FFA, the 75% Hard and 90% Impossible strongest-neighbor floors plus the 20% minimum-send check create readable constraints. They can make a strong neighboring player a kind of anchor that limits what the Nation normally sends elsewhere. That is still not protection: retaliation, changing troop counts, death or alliance of the anchor, and multiple attack states can alter the calculation. Read the floor as a reason to recalculate after a major battle, not as a permanent shield supplied by another player.

In Team, remove those two FFA assumptions from the sheet. Official tests show that weak attacks can proceed and the neighbor-based troop floor does not apply. Team relationships also change who is friendly, who is a valid bordering enemy, and which assistance request is processed. Coordinate stop lines with teammates: one player holds the Nation contact, one preserves a counterforce, and nobody opens another front without naming who covers it. The v34 normal Team win threshold is now 80%, matching FFA rather than the old 95%, so a long Nation cleanup should not distract from a nearer territory finish.

In Humans versus Nations or high-Nation custom games, parallel pressure makes fixed build orders even less reliable. The `juicy` comparison happens per Nation among its eligible border candidates, and Impossible can attack many Bots in parallel. A position can be low priority for one Nation and high priority for another. Draw one P-R-E-S-S row per contact. Collapse the front one Nation at a time when possible, but do not assume a 400-Nation challenge video proves a standard public-map solution. Those videos validate the player need for reserves, waiting, alternate bases, and single-front discipline; the current tag defines the rules.

Map scale changes the time available to react, not the core test. On a small map, a 30-to-49-tick Impossible interval and a short border can punish an exposed build quickly. On a large map, the same internal interval may still leave travel and route time, but an extra contact can be harder to close. The correct adjustment is how conservative the Stop line must be. The five questions remain stable because each input is visible or explicitly marked unknown.

## Keep a pressure ledger and verify the version boundary

The repeatable practice is a three-line ledger taken before expansion, after first contact, and after the Nation commits. Before expansion, write the Nation's current Troops, your current and maximum Troops, exposed structure levels, existing contacts, proposed new contacts, and the stop event. After contact, classify Pressure as cold, warm, or hot and record whether the capture improved the boundary. After commitment, note the outgoing target and force, then decide whether the window supports a bounded objective or only a refill. Short records expose which assumption failed without pretending that internal random state was observable.

| Ledger moment | Record | Continue only when | Stop or switch when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before expansion | Nation Troops, your Troops/cap, prize levels, borders, intended stop | The capture improves shape and leaves a named reserve | It exposes the economic core or adds an uncovered contact |
| After first contact | Eligibility estimate, new route, new neighbors, current pressure | The boundary is narrower and the next move remains reversible | Reward rises faster than safety or the Nation becomes hot |
| After Nation commitment | Target, visible force, remaining route, counter objective | One bounded objective finishes before the response can reset | The commitment ends, reserve crosses the stop line, or a flank opens |

The evidence boundary is OpenFront `v0.34.0-beta1`, checked on 11 September 2026. The official [Release][release] names the new `juicy()` strategy and the Terra Nullius/fallout troop-cap fix. The exact [AiAttackBehavior source][attack-source] defines eligibility, the three score inputs, priority order, reserve floors, weak-attack rejection, and exceptions. [NationExecution][nation-source] defines generated trigger, reserve, expansion ratios and difficulty reaction intervals. Official [AiAttackBehavior tests][attack-tests] prove that a richer underfilled target can outrank the weakest one, pin Hard at 75% and Impossible at 90% in FFA, cover the 20% rejection, and demonstrate Team and retaliation exceptions.

Community posts and videos in the linked research pack answer a different question: what players notice and which decisions repeatedly fail. They support the language of staying high, waiting for another attack, reducing contacts, moving to an alternate base, and investing behind safety. They do not prove current ratios, guaranteed spawn locations, or a universal build order. This separation is deliberate. If a later Release changes the Nation target score or send restraints, preserve P-R-E-S-S and recompute the thresholds from the new tag.

Make the first move small enough to observe the response. Stop when the declared signal appears even if land remains available. Resume only after a new P-R-E-S-S pass. That discipline cannot remove randomness, but it prevents the most common strategic error: turning a strong economy into the richest safe target on the Nation's border.
