## Direct answer: what does the Doomsday Clock do?

The **Doomsday Clock** ends stalled OpenFront matches by making every non-leading side hold a rising share of the map's usable land.

It is not a circle that moves toward the geographic center. After a 10-minute grace period, the required share ramps through 2%, 4%, 7%, 11%, 17%, 25%, and 35%.

If your side falls below the live bar, a skull appears and you get 30 seconds to recover. After that, troops and Warship health drain each second.

Climb back above the bar and the mark clears immediately.

If you cannot recover, your troop floor decays from 40% to 5% over the next 90 seconds. Once you are pinned at that floor, territory starts rotting and is scheduled to reach zero 150 seconds after the skull first appeared.

Your practical objective is therefore simple: hold a connected land share with a safety margin, keep enough troops to take cheap tiles during the warning, and become the territory leader before the final squeeze.

## First correct the biggest misconception

There is no hidden safe-zone location and no rule that checks distance from the center. The game compares tile counts.

In FFA, your own territory is your side's territory. In team modes, every living teammate's territory is added together and compared with the same bar used for a solo side.

A tiny teammate is safe when the team total is safe; if the team total is below the bar, every member receives the skull and decay.

The bar uses current land excluding fallout. The panel shows the required percentage and your side's percentage. Read that comparison instead of guessing which region of the map will remain safe.

## Read the panel before making the next move

The Doomsday panel appears when the mode is enabled. Public games show a modifier badge, including the speed when available; hosts can enable the mode and choose a pace in custom games.

The panel reports **Stable**, **Unstable**, **Collapsing**, or **Decaying**. Decaying is the red-skull territory-loss phase.

The panel also shows the territory percentage you must hold, your own or your team's share, the current wave target, and the time until the next rise.

Treat orange or near-danger feedback as an order to stop spending troops on optional fights.

The interface marks you as near danger when your share is within roughly 10% of the current requirement. A paper-thin lead over the line can disappear after one lost border.

## Wave schedule and speed presets

Every preset keeps the first ten minutes at 0%. The threshold then rises smoothly during each wave and pauses briefly at its target. It does not jump instantly from one listed percentage to the next.

| Speed | 2% | 4% | 7% | 11% | 17% | 25% | 35% final |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Slow | 14:00 | 19:10 | 24:20 | 29:30 | 34:40 | 39:50 | 45:00 |
| Normal | 12:48 | 16:30 | 20:12 | 23:54 | 27:36 | 31:18 | 35:00 |
| Fast | 11:42 | 13:55 | 16:08 | 18:21 | 20:34 | 22:47 | 25:00 |
| Very fast | 10:36 | 11:20 | 12:04 | 12:48 | 13:32 | 14:16 | 15:00 |

The listed time is when that wave reaches its full target. During the ramp, the live requirement can be any value between the previous and next targets.

At the final 35% bar, two sides can still both be safe. The leading side is exempt from Doomsday, but the threshold no longer forces a single survivor by arithmetic alone.

Territory rot closes the match by eliminating any non-leading side that stays below the bar for the full deadline.

That exemption leaves an army capable of closing the match instead of crippling everyone into another stalemate. Reaching this wave does not award an instant win; normal conquest, elimination, or timer rules still decide the result.

## Exact ramp starts and pause windows

The first table tells you when each target is fully reached. The table below tells you when each climb begins, which is the more useful moment for planning attacks and recovery routes.

| Speed | Ramps begin | Targets are fully reached | Pause after waves 1–6 |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Slow | 10:00 · 15:10 · 20:20 · 25:30 · 30:40 · 35:50 · 41:00 | 14:00 · 19:10 · 24:20 · 29:30 · 34:40 · 39:50 · 45:00 | 70 seconds |
| Normal | 10:00 · 13:42 · 17:24 · 21:06 · 24:48 · 28:30 · 32:12 | 12:48 · 16:30 · 20:12 · 23:54 · 27:36 · 31:18 · 35:00 | 54 seconds |
| Fast | 10:00 · 12:13 · 14:26 · 16:39 · 18:52 · 21:05 · 23:18 | 11:42 · 13:55 · 16:08 · 18:21 · 20:34 · 22:47 · 25:00 | 31 seconds |
| Very fast | 10:00 · 10:44 · 11:28 · 12:12 · 12:56 · 13:40 · 14:24 | 10:36 · 11:20 · 12:04 · 12:48 · 13:32 · 14:16 · 15:00 | 8 seconds |

The pause is your cleanest rebuilding window. The live bar stops moving, but enemies can still remove territory and push you below it.

Do not confuse a target's arrival with the next ramp. On Normal speed, for example, 7% is reached at 20:12, but 11% does not start climbing until 21:06.

## How the game calculates the safety line

The game converts the live percentage into a whole-tile requirement. In simplified form, the check is:

`required tiles = floor(live threshold × usable land tiles)`

Usable land is the map's land count minus tiles covered by fallout. Water does not enter this denominator, so owning sea routes or surrounding empty ocean cannot raise your Doomsday share.

Your side is safe when its tile count is **equal to or above** the requirement. Only a non-leading side strictly below the required tile count receives the warning.

In a team game, the game adds the living members' territory before making that comparison. It does not multiply the requirement by the number of teammates.

The current territory leader is checked separately and is exempt. A side can therefore be safe either by meeting the line or by holding first place.

### Worked threshold example

Suppose the map currently has 80,000 usable land tiles and the live bar is 12.5%. The whole-tile requirement is 10,000 tiles.

- A solo player with 10,000 tiles is safe because equality counts.
- A two-player team with 6,200 and 3,800 tiles is also safe because the combined total is 10,000.
- A non-leading side with 9,999 tiles receives the warning.
- A side with 9,999 tiles remains exempt if it is still the territory leader.

Fallout can change the absolute tile requirement because fallout land is removed from usable land. Treat that as a live denominator change, not as territory you captured.

## What happens after you fall below the bar

The sequence is recoverable at first and brutal if ignored.

| State | What the game does | Your best response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stable | Your side is above the bar, or you are the current leader | Build a 10–15% relative cushion over the requirement |
| Unstable | A non-leading side is below the bar; the 30-second warning is running | Cancel optional attacks and capture the cheapest reachable land |
| Collapsing | The warning expired; troops and Warships drain while their protected floor falls from 40% toward 5% | Recover territory immediately; stockpiled troops alone will not stop the countdown |
| Decaying | The floor has bottomed out and the red skull marks territory rot | Cross the bar now; land, routes, and structures are being removed toward the 150-second deadline |

Troop loss starts at 2% of maximum troop capacity per second and rises linearly to 5% over 90 seconds. At the same time, the protected troop floor falls linearly from 40% to 5%.

Because both values use maximum capacity rather than current troops, simply hoarding a full army does not solve the problem.

Warships start with gentler 1% health loss, then the curve steepens toward 50% per second.

Warships use the same falling 40%-to-5% floor against veterancy-adjusted maximum health. Healing is suppressed while their owner is doomed.

The skull's full deadline is 150 seconds. After the 30-second warning and 90-second floor-decay window, a side pinned at its floor enters territory rot.

The sim opens scattered holes, then spreads them through owned land. Unowned tiles no longer support routes or structures. The per-second quota adjusts so the remaining territory reaches zero at the deadline.

This is still recoverable until the side is eliminated. Reaching the bar or becoming the territory leader clears the skull, stops drain and rot immediately, and resets the rot path for a later failure.

## A 30-second recovery playbook

The warning is long enough for a prepared recovery and too short for improvising a distant war. Use the skull as a timed checklist.

### Seconds 0–5: stop making the gap worse

Cancel optional outbound attacks and read the exact percentage gap. Check whether the bar is still rising or is currently paused.

Do not spend the first seconds searching for the geographic center. Identify the cheapest land already connected to your borders or to a transport route that is ready now.

### Seconds 5–15: take the cheapest reliable land

Prefer neutral tiles, a weak border, or an exposed pocket that can finish quickly. The best move is the one that restores the percentage, not the one with the most impressive target.

In a team game, assign one player to capture and another to prevent a border collapse. Everyone shares the result, so two teammates should not duplicate the same attack.

### Seconds 15–25: protect the recovery route

If the first capture is insufficient, continue along the same cheap frontier. Switching to a new distant front wastes the warning on travel and setup.

Use the army and fleet to keep the route open. Troop donations can support the capture, but donations alone do not change the team's territory share.

### Seconds 25–30: choose territory over pride

Abandon a slow prestige attack if it cannot complete before the warning expires. A small safe capture is worth more than nearly winning a large battle.

If recovery is impossible during the warning, preserve two routes and avoid feeding units. The next goal is to cross the line during the 90-second falling-floor window, before territory rot starts deleting those routes.

### After decay starts

Treat 2:30 from the first skull as a hard elimination deadline, not a promise of exactly 90 seconds of usable troops.

Maximum capacity changes with territory, and rot begins only once the falling floor has reached 5% and the army is pinned to it.

Recovering the land line remains the direct reset. Once safe, the skull clears, drain and rot stop, and a later failure starts with a fresh 30-second warning and a fresh rot pattern.

## The 10-minute plan

Use the grace period for expansion and positioning, but do not treat it as ten free minutes of greed. Your territory shape at 10:00 determines how cheaply you can answer the first ramp.

Favor connected borders with several low-cost expansion options. A long tendril that reaches valuable land can look impressive while leaving only one expensive route for emergency growth.

Keep enough troops to take neutral or weak territory near the wave. Spending everything at 9:40 on a slow conquest can leave you below the first moving bar with no recovery tool.

Prepare two expansion directions. They do not need to point toward the map center; they need to reach territory you can actually take when the panel turns red.

## Four speed-specific game plans

The target percentages never change, but the time available for rebuilding changes radically. Select your plan from the speed badge rather than memorizing one universal opening.

### Slow: build depth, then convert it

Slow reaches 2% at 14:00 and does not begin the 35% ramp until 41:00. You have time for real wars, but repeated 70-second pauses can hide a bad long-term position.

Use the early game to build several layers of connected land. By 35:50, when the 25% ramp begins, stop treating remote holdings as permanent if they cannot support the final contest.

The danger on Slow is complacency. A player can look safe for half an hour, then discover that the same narrow border cannot supply enough land for 25% and 35%.

### Normal: finish each war before the next milestone

Normal begins the 7% ramp at 17:24, the 11% ramp at 21:06, the 25% ramp at 28:30, and the final 35% ramp at 32:12.

Use those four times as campaign deadlines. A war that is still consuming troops when the next climb begins must either deliver territory immediately or be canceled.

The 54-second pauses are long enough to refill a margin but too short for crossing the map. Keep the next capture option adjacent before each target arrives.

### Fast: combine expansion with every attack

Fast starts climbing to 11% at 16:39 and to 25% at 21:05. The final 35% ramp already begins at 23:18.

Every major attack should also improve your territory share or remove the opponent's recovery path. Pure troop trades are expensive because the next threshold arrives before armies fully rebuild.

Enter minute 20 with a route to first place. Waiting until the 35% ramp begins leaves little time to change the ranking or survive a 150-second doomed deadline.

### Very fast: the opening is the endgame

Very fast starts at 10:00 and reaches 35% at 15:00. Every pause is only 8 seconds, so the bar is moving for most of the five-minute squeeze.

Avoid long conquests before minute ten. Secure cheap land, a compact border, and a transport path before the first ramp begins.

At 13:40 the 25% ramp starts; at 14:24 the final 35% ramp starts. By then, you should already be contesting the territory lead rather than merely trying to survive.

## How to play each part of the squeeze

### Before the first ramp

Prioritize land efficiency over one prestige target. Secure borders that give several follow-up captures, and avoid creating an isolated pocket that needs transports or one hostile corridor to grow.

Check the speed badge. A Very fast match reaches 35% at 15:00, so every early detour is expensive. A Slow match gives more fighting time, but the same final threshold and rot deadline still arrive.

### During a ramp

Watch the live percentage, not only the target printed in the schedule. If the bar is climbing from 7% to 11%, being at 9% is temporarily safe but already on a deadline.

Prefer low-risk tile gains. A failed large attack can cost both the territory you hoped to win and the troop reserve needed to recover.

### During a pause

Use the flat interval to rebuild the safety margin. A pause is not proof that the danger passed; it is the preparation window before the next target begins rising.

If you are barely safe, improve the border before starting a distant war. If an opponent is barely safe, prepare a short attack that lands just before the next ramp.

### Near 25% and 35%

At 25%, only a few sides can remain comfortably above the same bar. Stop measuring success by survival alone and start measuring whether you can become the leader.

At 35%, two sides can still meet the bar, and the wave does not declare a winner. Territory rot, ordinary conquest, elimination, Ranked exceptions, or configured timers finish the match.

If you are not leading, a narrow buffer is temporary. Overtake the crown, remove the leader's land, or coordinate the decisive team push before the final requirement settles.

## FFA decision rules

The territory leader cannot be doomed, even if every remaining player is below the bar. This means taking first place can be more valuable than sitting just above the threshold.

When the leader is far ahead, attack land that changes the ranking rather than chasing a weak doomed player for no territorial gain. The clock is already weakening that player.

When you are leading, keep pressure on challengers without giving away the crown. You do not need to meet the bar while you remain the leader, but losing enough territory to fall into second can expose you immediately.

## Team-mode decision rules

Teams are judged on combined territory against the same requirement as one FFA player. The threshold is not multiplied by team size.

Split roles during a warning. One teammate can hold the enemy front while another captures cheap land. Because every member shares the team's status, personal tile count is less important than the combined total.

Do not abandon a small teammate just because their own territory is tiny. If the team is safe, they are safe. If the team is doomed, donations alone cannot clear the skull; someone must restore the shared territory percentage.

## Naval and island-map decisions

Water does not satisfy a land-share requirement. A powerful fleet can control movement while its owner still falls below the territory bar.

Use Warships to protect transports, deny enemy landings, and keep expansion routes open. Do not treat naval dominance as a substitute for owning land.

If your side becomes doomed, Warships keep patrolling but cannot heal and fall with the same 40%-to-5% protected-floor countdown. Recover the land threshold first; then the surviving fleet can repair and matter again.

## Choose territory by shape, not by distance from center

Two positions with the same percentage can have very different recovery chances. The useful question is how many cheap captures remain connected to the position.

| Territory shape | What it gives you | Doomsday risk | Best adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Broad connected frontier | Several neutral or weak directions | More borders to defend | Keep reserves near the cheapest two directions |
| Long narrow finger | Fast access to distant land | One cut can isolate the route | Widen the base before the next ramp |
| Peninsula or chokepoint | Efficient defense and predictable contact | Limited expansion once the neck closes | Cross or secure the neck before the warning |
| Archipelago holding | Strong transport and naval control | Water adds no territory share | Pre-position transports and protect landing tiles |
| Several isolated pockets | Multiple local fronts | Reinforcement and growth are fragmented | Connect pockets or abandon the least recoverable one |
| Compact interior block | Short defensive borders | Nearby cheap land may run out | Prepare a breakout before 17% and 25% |

The map center is valuable only when it offers connected, capturable land or changes the territory lead. Moving inward without a capture plan gives no special protection.

### When to abandon a rich edge

Do not leave an edge merely because it is far from the center. Leave it when its next useful capture requires a slow transport, an expensive corridor, or a war you cannot finish before the next ramp.

Conversely, a rich edge with several weak neighbors can be safer than a central pocket surrounded by strong players. The threshold rewards land access, not map aesthetics.

### When to keep a narrow corridor

A corridor is worth holding when it connects two expansion basins or protects the only transport landing. It is a liability when all recovery depends on one fortified enemy tile.

Before a ramp, either widen the corridor, station enough troops to prevent a cut, or prepare a second route. Do not discover its fragility during the 30-second warning.

## How to use the clock against an opponent

Target the opponent who is close to the line and is not the leader. A small border loss can trigger their 30-second warning and force them to cancel a planned attack.

Time pressure is strongest near the start of a ramp. Take or neutralize cheap edge territory just before the requirement rises, then contest the routes they need for recovery.

Do not overextend to doom someone who can reclaim easier land elsewhere. The best setup removes both current territory and the opponent's cheapest recovery path.

If the target is the leader, the clock will not punish them. First overtake their territory total or help another side do so; only a non-leading side can be marked.

## Common ways players lose

### Treating Doomsday like a geographic circle

Running toward the visual center does nothing by itself. The mechanic checks territory share, not coordinates. Move toward capturable, defensible land.

### Waiting for the skull

The skull means you are already below the requirement. Use the wave timer and percentage gap to act before the warning begins.

### Holding exactly the required share

One lost border can erase a tiny margin. Aim for a cushion, especially while the bar is still ramping.

### Spending every troop on a long attack

Decay is stopped by territory recovery, not by winning a slow battle eventually. Preserve a fast capture option.

### Assuming a fleet keeps you safe

Warships help secure routes, but sea control does not count as land. A doomed navy also loses healing and health.

### Looking only at your personal territory in teams

The panel and simulation use the team's combined tiles. Coordinate the shared number and give each player a role.

### Chasing a doomed player instead of the leader

The clock already cripples and eventually rots non-leading sides. If the crown remains untouched, the final wave favors the leader you ignored.

## Three practical examples

### Normal speed at 21:30

The threshold is ramping from 7% toward 11%, which completes at 23:54. Your side holds 9%. Cancel the distant attack, take nearby weak land, and rebuild a margin before the 17% wave begins.

### Fast team game at 19:40

The bar is ramping from 11% toward 17%, which completes at 20:34. Your team holds 16%; the rival team holds 15%.

One teammate pressures their border while the other captures safe land, trying to keep your total above the live line while pushing theirs below it.

### Very fast island game at 14:00

The 25% target arrives at 14:16, and 35% arrives at 15:00. Your fleet controls the sea but your land share is 22%. Use transports for immediate land, because another Warship does not change the Doomsday calculation.

## Doomsday Clock FAQ

### Can the clock eliminate me by itself?

Yes in v33.2. Drain itself respects the falling floor, but territory rot then removes owned land toward zero and can eliminate the side 150 seconds after the skull first appeared.

Crossing the bar or becoming leader before elimination stops the process.

### Is holding exactly the required tile count safe?

Yes. The danger check is strictly below the whole-tile requirement. Equality is safe, although one lost tile may immediately remove that safety.

### Does ocean or naval control count toward the bar?

No. The calculation uses usable land. A fleet matters only because it protects transports, landing tiles, and access to land.

### Does fallout count as land I still need to hold?

Fallout tiles are removed from the usable-land denominator. They do not become owned territory, and they can change the absolute number of tiles required by the same percentage.

### Can troop donations clear a team's skull?

Not by themselves. Donations can help a teammate capture land, but the skull clears only when the combined team territory reaches the line or the team becomes the leader.

### Can doomed Warships heal at a Port?

No. Passive and docked healing are suppressed while the owner is doomed. The ships keep patrolling and can heal again after the side recovers.

### Does becoming the leader reset the warning?

Yes. The leading side is exempt, so becoming the territory leader clears the mark and stops drain and rot. Losing first place can expose the side again.

### Can a custom-game host change the warning or drain rates?

The lobby exposes whether Doomsday is enabled and which speed preset is used. The 30-second warning, drain curves, 40%-to-5% floor decay, and 150-second rot deadline are fixed configuration values rather than separate lobby sliders.

### Does the final 35% wave declare the leader the winner?

No. Two sides can still meet 35% at once. The final bar narrows the field, while territory rot, normal conquest, elimination, Ranked exceptions, or configured timers determine the result.
