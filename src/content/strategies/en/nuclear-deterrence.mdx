## Direct Answer

Nuclear deterrence means preserving a credible reply while making an enemy's first strike expensive or unreliable. In OpenFront, that requires Gold, a Silo, protected launch capacity, and SAM coverage that matches the assets at risk.

One SAM is warning and basic coverage, not complete safety. Its 90-tick cooldown creates a throughput limit. In v33 beta, the MIRV carrier is not a SAM target, but separated warheads follow the normal trajectory and timing checks.

Choose a weapon by mission. Atom Bombs are cheaper and smaller. Hydrogen Bombs cover a much larger area. MIRVs carry many smaller warheads across the main SAM screen, but the warheads become normal in-flight targets after separation.

Do not launch only because a weapon is available. A strike should remove capacity, open a ground attack, deny a route, or force a defensive expense that improves your position.

## Prerequisite Mechanics

### Missile Silo

The first Missile Silo costs a fixed 1,000,000 Gold and takes 100 ticks to construct. It is the launch platform for nuclear weapons.

Silo upgrades reduce reload time. Multiple Silos can support more launch capacity, but every exposed Silo is also a point an opponent can capture with its territory.

A Silo without a funded weapon is still a signal. A Silo without protection, Gold, or a survivable core is not yet a reliable deterrent.

### Atom Bomb

An Atom Bomb costs 750,000 Gold. Its listed blast radii are 12 tiles for the inner area and 30 for the outer area.

SAM Launchers can intercept it. Its lower cost makes repeated launches affordable sooner, but repeated shots still meet SAM cooldown and coverage rules.

### Hydrogen Bomb

A Hydrogen Bomb costs 5,000,000 Gold. Its listed inner radius is 80 tiles and its outer radius is 100.

It can also be intercepted by SAM. The larger area makes asset spacing and interception coverage more important than against an Atom Bomb.

### MIRV

The first MIRV costs 25,000,000 Gold. The formula adds 15,000,000 for each prior MIRV launch, so the second costs 40,000,000.

The carrier splits into multiple MIRV warheads. Each warhead lists inner and outer radii of 12 and 18 tiles.

The MIRV carrier cannot be intercepted.

After separation, the [v0.33.0-beta1 SAM execution](https://github.com/openfrontio/OpenFrontIO/blob/v0.33.0-beta1/src/core/execution/SAMLauncherExecution.ts) evaluates each warhead through the same trajectory, range, timing, ready-shot, and cooldown logic as other supported nukes.

Launching MIRV also puts the actual Missile Silo on cooldown. An immediate follow-up therefore needs another ready Silo or a later firing window.

Carrier immunity does not make a MIRV an automatic finisher. Warhead trajectories, its price, smaller warhead radius, target spread, and the defender's ability to reclaim ground all matter.

### SAM Launcher

The first SAM costs 1,500,000 Gold. The cost formula is `min(3,000,000, (n+1) × 1,500,000)`, so later SAMs reach a 3,000,000 per-build cap.

Construction takes 300 ticks, or about 30 seconds. A SAM cannot solve a launch that is already arriving if construction has not finished.

Cooldown is 90 ticks, about nine seconds. A single launcher cannot provide unlimited interception against closely timed Atom or Hydrogen Bombs.

Range follows `150 - 480 / (level + 5)`. The first levels are 70, about 81, 90, about 97, and 102 tiles. Higher levels continue toward the 150-tile limit.

Interception also needs a nonnegative pre-fire window: `nukeReachTick - samReachTick >= 0`. Coverage on the map is necessary, but the missile must still reach the path in time.

### Cities as strategic depth

Each City level adds 250,000 troop cap. That fixed buffer does not intercept a weapon, yet it can leave more capacity for defense and recovery after losses.

Cities, Silos, SAMs, and Ports can transfer when their territory is captured. Nuclear defense therefore includes the land around the network, not just the launchers.

## Decision Framework

### Step 1: define what must survive

List the assets whose loss would end your plan: launch Silos, high-level Cities, key Ports, factories, a narrow ground connection, or the only safe expansion lane.

Do not cover empty territory because it looks central. Place coverage around the assets and routes that preserve retaliation or recovery.

### Step 2: identify the incoming weapon set

If the opponent owns a Silo but has limited Gold, Atom Bombs are the cheaper threat. At 5,000,000 Gold, a Hydrogen Bomb becomes possible. At 25,000,000, the first MIRV becomes possible.

Gold is not always visible or exact, so use these as capability thresholds, not proof that a weapon exists.

### Step 3: separate coverage from redundancy

Coverage asks whether a target lies within SAM range. Redundancy asks whether another completed SAM can engage while the first is cooling down.

Two SAM circles that overlap a core can improve salvo defense. If both launchers also fit inside one 100-tile Hydrogen outer radius, one successful strike may threaten the whole network.

Spread the launchers while preserving overlap on the important target. There is no single spacing number that works for every map geometry and SAM level.

### Step 4: preserve a retaliation budget

The first Silo plus first SAM costs 2,500,000 Gold before buying a weapon. Adding an Atom Bomb raises that package to 3,250,000.

A protected Hydrogen capability needs at least 7,500,000 for one first Silo, one first SAM, and the bomb, before upgrades or redundancy.

Do not spend the entire treasury on the visible weapon if doing so prevents the SAM, City, or ground defense that keeps the launcher alive.

### Step 5: choose a strike mission

Use a small strike for a small objective. Use a large blast when the target density justifies the cost. Use a MIRV when dispersed trajectories can create more value than the defender's ready SAM coverage can remove.

Before launch, state the follow-up in one sentence. If you cannot explain what changes after impact, keep the weapon as leverage or choose another investment.

## Timeline and Step-by-Step Execution

### Before nuclear spending

Secure enough territory and economy to fund the package, not just one component. Check whether nukes or SAMs are disabled before reserving Gold for unavailable systems.

Keep critical buildings separated from the border. A nuclear network that can be captured by a normal land push is not a nuclear solution.

### Deterrence setup

Build the Silo where it can remain inside the defended core. Start the first SAM early enough to finish its 300-tick construction before the expected threat window.

Place the SAM by drawing an imagined circle around the assets that must survive. The first-level 70-tile range is the planning baseline.

Do not advertise an empty treasury through overbuilding. Keep enough Gold to buy the intended weapon or to react with another defensive structure.

### First completed shield

When the SAM finishes, confirm which Cities, Silos, and routes are actually inside range. A launcher icon is not proof that the whole core is covered.

Check the approach geometry. The pre-fire formula means interception depends on reach time as well as the target sitting inside a nominal circle.

If AI opponents are present, existing structure notes say AI nukes avoid SAM-covered targets. That adds deterrence value, but it does not change human targeting, carrier immunity, or the normal post-separation warhead checks.

### Redundancy phase

Add a second SAM when the first protects something that cannot survive a cooldown gap. The second launcher costs 3,000,000 under the listed scaling formula.

Offset the second position so both can cover the core without placing every launcher and Silo in one compact blast area.

Upgrade range only when the added circle covers a real asset or intercept path. Range purchased without a coverage purpose is expensive decoration.

### Strike preparation

Verify the target, enemy SAM circles, likely ground follow-up, and your own retaliation risk. Keep the weapon unlaunched if the impact would not change those conditions.

For Atom or Hydrogen Bombs, assume covered paths may be intercepted. For MIRV, assume the carrier reaches separation.

Then trace whether each important warhead path crosses a ready launcher's range with enough time for the interceptor to arrive.

### Launch and follow-through

Launch when troops, allies, or naval units can exploit the new opening. A crater without follow-up can become neutral space that the opponent recovers.

After launch, watch Silo reload, enemy construction, SAM cooldowns, and your Gold. Do not repeat the same target automatically if the first strike changed the geometry.

### Recovery after an incoming strike

Rebuild the capability that the strike was meant to remove. If a SAM fell, restore coverage. If a City fell, replace troop cap. If a route opened, secure the exposed land.

In Water Nukes mode, re-expansion timing also matters because unowned destroyed land can convert to water. Use the dedicated guide for that option's exact behavior.

## Number Scenarios

### Scenario 1: minimum visible deterrent

One first Silo costs 1,000,000 and one first SAM costs 1,500,000, for 2,500,000 total. Their listed construction times are 100 and 300 ticks.

Adding one Atom Bomb costs another 750,000, bringing the package to 3,250,000. This creates launch capacity, one weapon, and one layer of interceptable-nuke defense.

It does not create salvo redundancy or guaranteed wide-area MIRV defense. It does let one ready launcher engage a separated warhead when path and timing permit; value still depends on placement and readiness.

### Scenario 2: overlapping SAM cost

The first SAM costs 1,500,000. The second costs 3,000,000, so two launchers total 4,500,000 before upgrades.

A third also costs 3,000,000 because the formula caps there. Three unupgraded launchers therefore cost 7,500,000.

That budget buys more interception throughput, but only where the circles and pre-fire windows cover the incoming path.

### Scenario 3: range by level

Using the generated formula, levels one through five are 70, about 81, 90, about 97, and 102 tiles.

The gain per level shrinks as range approaches 150. Upgrade because a specific asset or route enters coverage, not because the level number looks low.

### Scenario 4: two Atom Bombs versus one Hydrogen Bomb

Two Atom Bombs cost 1,500,000 and each has 12/30 inner/outer radii. One Hydrogen Bomb costs 5,000,000 and has 80/100 radii.

The cheaper pair offers two launch events and two possible targets. The Hydrogen Bomb offers a far larger single blast. SAMs can intercept either weapon type.

The better choice depends on target density, coverage, cooldown timing, and the objective. Cost alone does not make the weapons interchangeable.

### Scenario 5: MIRV escalation

The first MIRV costs 25,000,000. After one launch, the next costs 40,000,000. Buying both would consume 65,000,000 before Silo or defense costs.

That escalation rewards selective use. The carrier bypasses SAM targeting, but separated warheads can be intercepted, the listed blasts are only 12/18, the Silo enters cooldown, and v24 craters can be reclaimed quickly.

### Scenario 6: City buffer

One City level adds 250,000 troop cap. Two levels add 500,000, and four levels add 1,000,000.

This does not reduce blast size. It gives the broader position more capacity to field troops before and after a strike, provided the City itself remains controlled.

## Opponent Counterplay

### SAM saturation

An enemy can time multiple interceptable weapons close together. A single SAM has a 90-tick cooldown, so coverage without another ready launcher can leave a later shot unanswered.

Counter by overlapping completed launchers on critical assets, while avoiding a cluster that one successful Hydrogen Bomb can threaten.

### MIRV saturation

SAMs cannot intercept the carrier, but they can engage separated warheads through normal trajectory checks.

Counter with overlapping coverage on critical paths, asset spacing, multiple recoverable cores, preserved ground forces, and enough economy to rebuild.

Do not keep upgrading one SAM as if range alone guarantees every interception. The broader MIRV answer is overlapping launchers, ready shots, resilience, and recovery.

### Target dispersion

An opponent can separate Cities, Silos, Ports, and SAMs so one blast hits fewer valuable assets. That reduces the return on a Hydrogen Bomb.

Counter by choosing a narrower objective, coordinating several fronts, or keeping the weapon as leverage instead of forcing a low-value shot.

### Ground capture

Silos and SAMs transfer with captured territory, while Defense Posts are destroyed. A land attack can dismantle a nuclear network without winning the interception contest.

Protect the surrounding border and keep troops available. Nuclear defense that ignores ordinary capture has a hole no launcher can cover.

### Economic denial

Opponents may attack Ports, rail routes, or territory to keep you below the next weapon threshold. A 25,000,000 MIRV plan is fragile if it depends on one exposed income stream.

Counter with mixed income, protected infrastructure, and a lower-cost option when the original budget is no longer realistic.

### Reclaiming the crater

The v24 MIRV note says craters can be conquered quickly. Any strike without troops ready to exploit the opening may give the defender time to restore control.

Coordinate the launch with the unit or border move that benefits from the impact.

## Failure Modes

### Naked Silo

A Silo outside SAM coverage and near a weak border offers visible value to both nuclear and land attacks. Put launch capacity inside the defended system.

### One-SAM confidence

One launcher adds real coverage, especially against AI targeting, but its cooldown can expose later weapons and MIRV can split across several paths. Label it a first layer, not a complete shield.

### Compact nuclear district

Clustering every City, Silo, and SAM simplifies defense until a large blast reaches all of them. Preserve overlap without creating one point of failure.

### Last-second SAM construction

Build time is 300 ticks. Starting after the warning appears may leave the launcher unfinished when the weapon arrives.

### Blind Atom spam

Several Atom Bombs can create multiple launch events, but covered shots may be intercepted. Count enemy launchers, cooldowns, paths, and the value of each target.

### MIRV as automatic victory

SAM immunity guarantees no SAM interception, not permanent control. Smaller warhead radii, high cost, spread, and reclaimable craters limit the result.

### Spending to zero

A weapon that empties the treasury can leave no answer to a counterattack. Keep a post-strike budget for troops, coverage, or replacement infrastructure.

### No follow-up

Damage is not the same as strategic gain. If no land, naval, or allied move uses the opening, the defender may rebuild or re-expand.

## Mode and Map Adjustments

### Nukes disabled

Do not build a nuclear plan around unavailable weapons. Redirect the same Gold analysis toward Cities, Posts, trade, trains, or naval control.

### SAMs disabled

Atom and Hydrogen Bombs lose their normal interceptor. Spread assets, maintain multiple cores, and avoid making one dense target worth the enemy's entire budget.

### Water Nukes enabled

Destroyed unowned land can convert to water after the strike. The blast sizes stay the same, but the map may gain permanent water and new naval routes.

Read [the Water Nukes guide](/guides/water-nukes/) before using a normal-mode recovery plan.

### FFA

Deterrence includes diplomacy. A visible reply can discourage one neighbor while another front remains active, but an alliance still has a five-minute duration and betrayal rules.

### Team modes

Split roles and call targets. One player can fund launch capacity while teammates provide ground follow-up, but a centralized arsenal also becomes a clear enemy objective.

### Tight regional maps

A 100-tile Hydrogen outer radius covers more of a compact core. Spacing may be constrained, so preserving a second core or another route becomes more valuable.

### Large continental maps

One SAM circle may cover only one cluster. Build zones around real assets instead of trying to paint the whole territory with coverage.

Use the [map database](/database/maps/) to confirm the map set, then judge distance and topology in the live match.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest nuclear deterrent?

A first Silo plus first SAM costs 2,500,000 Gold before a weapon. Adding an Atom Bomb raises the listed package to 3,250,000.

### How much does a MIRV cost in OpenFront?

The first MIRV costs 25,000,000 Gold. Each prior MIRV launch adds 15,000,000, so the second costs 40,000,000 and the third costs 55,000,000.

### Can one SAM stop every nuke?

No. It intercepts Atom and Hydrogen Bombs, cannot intercept the MIRV carrier, and can engage only one supported in-flight target before its 90-tick cooldown.

Separated MIRV warheads are supported targets, but path and timing still decide the shot.

### What range does a first SAM have?

The range formula gives 70 tiles at level one. Level two is about 81, level three is 90, and later levels continue toward 150.

### Should I cluster two SAMs for overlap?

Overlap critical assets, but separate launchers enough that one successful large blast does not threaten the entire network. Exact spacing depends on map geometry and levels.

### Atom Bomb or Hydrogen Bomb?

Choose Atom for a cheaper, smaller mission or multiple launch events. Choose Hydrogen when the 80/100 blast area creates enough value to justify 5,000,000 Gold.

### Does SAM stop a MIRV?

Partly. The carrier cannot be intercepted. After separation, each warhead becomes a normal supported target, so range, trajectory, interceptor travel time, ready shots, and cooldown decide what gets through.

### Is a MIRV always better than a Hydrogen Bomb?

No. The carrier crosses SAM targeting, but separated warheads can be intercepted. The first costs 25,000,000, later launches escalate, the firing Silo enters cooldown, each warhead is smaller, and v24 craters can be reclaimed quickly.

### Where should I target?

Target the asset or route whose loss enables a concrete follow-up. Dense Cities, launch capacity, trade infrastructure, and narrow connections can matter, but inspect coverage first.

### When should I launch?

Launch when the impact changes control, capacity, income, or defensive spending. If no follow-up exists, continued deterrence may be worth more.
