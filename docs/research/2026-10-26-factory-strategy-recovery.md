## Evidence notes and corrections

The old factory purpose article contained several attractive but unsupported shortcuts. Those included a fixed four-level maximum, a deterministic departure interval, a universal income estimate, and simplified network requirements. The replacement explains the useful causal chain first: a factory originates trains, an eligible connected city or port provides a paying stop, and a factory stop itself is not a paying destination. A factory cluster without a valid destination does not solve its problem by adding levels. This distinction appears in the first visible answer as well as the detailed article, so readers do not need to scroll through advanced economy discussion to understand the building.

The station payment rules are kept separate from route selection. Normal base payment differs for self, team or other, and allied stations. A foreign station owner can receive the matching payment; this is not a pot split into two halves. The reduction applies after the initial paid stops, with a floor, and depends on the number of paid stops rather than geographical distance. The article avoids promising that a train visits every city in a cluster, that a loop resets its payment history, or that adding a city level multiplies that station's base payout. Such promises were not supported by the examined execution path.

The network explanation distinguishes nearby-station discovery from rail path validation. The search radius is 110, the accepted individual path limit is approximately 155.56, and nearby stations separated by 15 or less are skipped for that connection. These are local connection rules, not a total journey limit. Intermediate stations can extend a network. Factories introduce nearby stations when completed, while cities and ports need suitable factory proximity to join through the relevant building behavior. The player-facing explanation uses previews and completed journeys as the practical checks instead of asking players to reason from source class names.

Departure behavior required a particularly important correction. The code checks total global Train units, including the units that form trains, for saturation. It does not use the player's soldier count. The midpoint near 560 Train units is part of a smooth damping function, not a hard cap. Factory levels affect the departure checks, and a successful departure is followed by a local minimum cooldown. The small-economy boost depends on total factory levels and fades by six levels; it is not the obsolete fleet-size calculation or a reward for having fewer than fifteen soldiers. The articles therefore describe probabilities and conditions instead of a fixed timer.

The old count article built nearly every section around the false premise that two factories are generally optimal because an individual army crosses a 560-unit threshold. Correcting only the headline or one number would have left the practical advice wrong. It was rewritten around three distinct decisions: whether a new position adds a useful origin, whether upgrading an existing position solves the actual need, and whether the current price and defensive reserve permit the investment. A third factory can be a normal economic purchase when its route is useful. No special endgame exception is required. The shorter structure removes unsupported historical claims rather than replacing them with repetitive filler.

The cost examples now consistently use the shared Port and Factory level progression. The early normal steps are 125,000, 250,000, 500,000, and a 1,000,000 per-step cap. One level-one factory plus one level-one port means the next relevant purchase is the third step, not another 250,000 purchase. Two factories and two ports from zero, without upgrades or ownership changes, total 1,875,000. A price cap is not a building level cap. Upgrades are not described as intrinsically cheaper per level than new buildings. Normal construction is 20 ticks for factories and cities, 50 for ports, and those durations are not income payback times.
